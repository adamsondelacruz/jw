Dear Brother Martin,

Warm Christian greetings.

As requested, attached is the service-committee-signed S-51 for the proposed Ashburton Tagalog Congregation. We respectfully submit it for your review and further direction.

To provide additional context for the recommendation, the following information helps demonstrate both the size of the local Tagalog-speaking field and the spiritual response within it.

**Local Filipino and Tagalog-speaking population**

- Statistics New Zealand's 2023 Census data, presented by Figure.NZ, records **1,605 Filipino people in Ashburton District**, approximately **4.6 percent of the district's population**.
- Figure.NZ language data from Statistics New Zealand's 2023 Census shows that approximately **2.17 percent of Ashburton District residents can speak Tagalog**, compared with 1.77 percent in Christchurch City, 1.64 percent in Auckland, and 1.16 percent in Selwyn District. Although Ashburton is much smaller than these centres, its Tagalog-speaking population is proportionally more concentrated—even than Auckland and Christchurch. This supports the view that Ashburton has one of the strongest Tagalog-speaking fields in New Zealand.
- Kristine Kaye Asuncion, president of the Federation of Dairy Workers of New Zealand (FDWNZ) and a member of the Mid Canterbury Federated Farmers Association, estimates that there are approximately **3,000 Filipinos in Mid Canterbury**. This is an informed local estimate rather than an official census figure, and it covers a wider area than Ashburton District. Nevertheless, that wider area overlaps substantially with the proposed Ashburton Tagalog territory and suggests that the potential field extends beyond the district-only census count.

Figure.NZ sources:

- Filipino population by territorial authority, 2023 Census: https://figure.nz/chart/kTHySRszQMlknOp2
- Proportion able to speak Tagalog by territorial authority, 2023 Census: https://figure.nz/chart/6zVZjwr8WNRNyJ9x

**Current spiritual activity and congregation resources**

- The August group proposal lists **35 publishers**, of whom **31 are active**. This represents an increase from the 33 publishers represented during the June circuit-overseer visit.
- The group has **10 regular pioneers**, **5 elders**, and **2 ministerial servants**, providing a stable core of experienced publishers and appointed brothers.
- The June S-303 recorded **46 Bible studies**. Separately supplied information identifies **34 progressive Bible studies**; the reporting period for that figure can be confirmed if needed.
- The June circuit-overseer visit recorded average attendance of **40 at the weekend meeting** and **38 at the midweek meeting**. Both figures are higher than the number of active publishers, indicating participation by Bible students, interested ones, and others in addition to the publishers.

These current figures are consistent with earlier evaluations of the field. The 2018 circuit-overseer report observed that there was “a real need for this language group in the Ashburton” and that the response of Filipino people in the ministry was excellent. The 2021 branch evaluation also referred to the significant Tagalog-speaking population and to interested ones who were studying and progressing toward discipleship.

We recognise that Filipino ethnicity alone does not establish dependence on Tagalog. However, the official population data, the informed local estimate, the number of active publishers and appointed brothers, the Bible-study activity, and the meeting attendance together indicate a substantial and productive language field. They also suggest that there are publishers and interested ones who benefit from receiving spiritual instruction and shepherding in Tagalog.

The signed S-51 presently proposes beginning in **November 2026** and records **NZ-1B** as the recommended circuit and section. Please let us know of any corrections, additional information, or further steps needed before the recommendation is forwarded.

Thank you very much for your assistance and direction.

Warm Christian love,

Adamson dela Cruz

Bottom line

Do not begin by asking a pioneer to step down. First verify the record, understand the person’s circumstances and offer help. A total below 560 hours at service-year end requires a prompt, balanced Congregation Service Committee determination; it is not automatic deletion. If discontinuation is best, present it as a potentially temporary, stress-relieving adjustment—not as discipline or a judgment of spiritual worth.

Thresholds and what they mean

Measure Figure Direction Reference
Annual requirement 600 hours An applicant should have organised personal affairs so the annual requirement can realistically be reached. SFG 14:1(3)
Review indicator Consistently below an average of 50 hours per month After factoring in hour credit, the service overseer and group overseer meet with the pioneer to offer assistance and assess whether the annual requirement is realistic. SFG 14:5
Year-end continuation benchmark At least 560 hours Hours in field service plus applicable hour credit; the pioneer may continue. SFG 14:6
Year-end decision point Below 560 hours The Service Committee promptly decides whether the pioneer should continue, using balanced judgment. This is not an automatic deletion rule. SFG 14:6-7

Important: 600 hours is the annual requirement; 560 hours is the separate year-end benchmark stated in Shepherd for continuation. Do not replace either figure with a locally invented rule.

Provisions and circumstances to distinguish

Situation Criteria Possible outcome Reference
Approved-project special consideration The pioneer missed the annual requirement because extensive work on approved theocratic projects exceeded the hour credit available. The pioneer may continue, should be warmly commended and has the special consideration noted on S-21. SFG 14:8
Infirm regular pioneer Rare provision: exemplary, over 50 years old, at least 15 cumulative years pioneering and a heartfelt desire to continue; the circuit overseer must be consulted. If the body approves, the pioneer may serve without an hour requirement; two Service Committee members explain the provision and it is noted on S-21. SFG 14:9
Ordinary changed circumstances Health, employment, family responsibilities or another adverse circumstance makes the annual requirement unrealistic but does not meet a special provision. Temporary discontinuation may be in the person’s best interests and relieve pressure until circumstances change. SFG 14:6-7

How to conduct the conversation

Principles

  • Use the meeting to listen and assist, not to announce a predetermined outcome.
  • Separate the privilege of regular pioneering from the person’s spirituality and value to Jehovah or the congregation.
  • Use verified figures without accusatory expressions such as ‘you failed to make your time.’
  • Do not pressure the pioneer to chase an unsafe or unrealistic deficit.
  • If discontinuation is considered, use the official idea of discontinuing for a time rather than the status-oriented expression ‘step down.’

Questions that invite an honest assessment

  • How have you been, and what circumstances affected your ministry during the service year?
  • Is any report or approved hour credit missing from the record?
  • What practical assistance could the elders or congregation provide?
  • Looking at your present circumstances, is reaching the annual requirement during the new service year realistic?
  • Would a workable adjustment make continuation sustainable, or would discontinuing for a time relieve pressure until circumstances change?

Suggested opening

We appreciate your service and want this to be an encouraging conversation, not a disciplinary one. Your value to Jehovah and the congregation is not measured by a title or a number of hours. We would first like to understand what affected your service, confirm that the record is accurate and see what practical help we can offer. Then we can consider together whether continuing regular pioneer service is realistic in your present circumstances.

Avoid

  • Beginning with a predetermined request that the person discontinue.
  • Saying, “You failed to make your time,” or implying that the congregation was let down.
  • Equating hours or a pioneer appointment with Jehovah’s approval of the person.
  • Pressuring the pioneer to pursue an unsafe or unrealistic catch-up schedule.
  • Leaving a year-end case below 560 hours unresolved.

If discontinuation is determined

  1. Obtain and consider the appropriate group overseer’s comments.
  2. Check whether special consideration or the infirm-pioneer provision applies.
  3. The Congregation Service Committee makes the determination using balanced judgment.
  4. Update the body of elders before any announcement.
  5. Two Service Committee members inform the pioneer personally before the announcement.
  6. Use the current JW Hub procedure and only the prescribed announcement.
  7. Continue warm shepherding; a temporary discontinuation is not discipline and does not diminish the person’s faithful service.

Ashburton transition application

  • Who handles the completed service year: Until 1 November 2026, the pioneer remains under the current congregation, so that congregation’s Service Committee should resolve the completed service-year review.
  • What the transfer letter must resolve: The letter of introduction should plainly state whether the existing body recommends that the pioneer continue.
  • What the new congregation does: If the recommendation is favourable, the new Ashburton Tagalog Congregation Service Committee confirms that the pioneer wishes to continue and, absent an exceptional reason, arranges the announcement at the next midweek meeting.
  • Reference: SFG 14:4; approval letter p. 2

Practical control: Resolve or clearly document each regular pioneer’s recommendation before the 1 November transfer. The new congregation should not inherit an ambiguous pioneer status.

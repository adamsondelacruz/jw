# Tagalog Group Email - Condensed Outline

## Purpose

- Thank the brothers for raising the matter.
- Acknowledge the desire to simplify and protect the elder body from unsustainable workload.
- Ask that the local spiritual need be carefully weighed before any conclusion is reached.

## Main Point

- The question is not only whether Filipino/Tagalog-speaking ones can function in English.
- The more important question is whether Tagalog helps them:
  - understand the truth accurately,
  - progress spiritually,
  - feel shepherded,
  - and remain stable during adjustment to life in New Zealand.

## Local Census Data

- Ashburton District had 1,605 Filipino people in the 2023 Census, about 4.6% of the district population.
- Total Asian population in Ashburton was 3,273, so Filipinos make up nearly half of the Asian population locally.
- Figure.NZ language data shows Ashburton District has about 2.17% Tagalog speakers.
- Comparison:
  - Christchurch City: about 1.77%
  - Auckland: about 1.64%
  - Selwyn: about 1.16%
- Suggested wording:
  - "Proportionally, Ashburton appears to have a notably concentrated Tagalog-speaking field, even compared with larger centres."

## Why This Matters

- The Filipino/Tagalog field appears distinct in Ashburton because of its size and concentration.
- It may not be simply one small nationality among many.
- It may be one of the more significant non-English-speaking fields in our local territory.

## Previous Evaluations

- 2018 circuit overseer report said:
  - "The spirit of the group is excellent."
  - "There is a real need for this language group in the Ashburton."
  - "The response of the Filipino people in the ministry is excellent."
  - There was "much more potential to grow the number of Bible Studies being conducted."
- 2021 Branch evaluation referred to a "significant Tagalog-speaking population."
- The Branch noted interested ones were "studying the Bible and making good progress toward becoming disciples."
- The Branch approved the recommendation that the group continue to function.

## Direction and Principles

- Shepherd direction on multilanguage fields focuses on helping interested individuals and publishers who are dependent on another language.
- The purpose is not to promote a language or culture.
- The purpose is to help people understand the truth accurately and progress spiritually.
- Elders are encouraged to know the needs in the congregation's territory and congregation, including how many interested individuals and publishers are dependent on the language.
- Shepherding principles also support taking an active interest in the spiritual, emotional, and physical needs of others.

## Balance

- The elder body's workload is a valid concern.
- Simplification may be needed.
- However, territory care and search work may be part of the normal responsibility of caring for a foreign-language field.
- The better question may be how to support the arrangement more sustainably, rather than whether the need still exists.

## Suggested Decision Frame

- Before discontinuing the group, ask:
  - Does the Tagalog field represent a unique local spiritual opportunity?
  - Who currently depends on or significantly benefits from Tagalog?
  - Has the need diminished, remained, or grown?
  - Could a lighter or more sustainable arrangement care for the need?
  - What would happen spiritually to current publishers, students, and interested ones if the group ended?

## Closing Thought

- The available data and previous evaluations suggest the need has not clearly diminished.
- The Filipino/Tagalog-speaking presence in Ashburton appears significant and continuing.
- The body can still prayerfully decide the best arrangement, but the decision should weigh both spiritual need and sustainable elder support.

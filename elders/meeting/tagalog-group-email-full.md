# Tagalog Group Email - Full Draft

Hi Brothers,

Thank you for raising this for consideration. I appreciate the spirit behind it, especially the concern for the well-being of the elder body and the desire to simplify where possible. I agree that we need to be realistic about what we can sustain and what will best help us care for the whole congregation.

At the same time, I feel it would be good for us to carefully weigh the local spiritual need before reaching any conclusion regarding the Tagalog group.

One point that stood out to me is that the Filipino/Tagalog field in Ashburton appears to be quite distinct compared with other ethnic or language groups in our territory. According to 2023 Census data summarized by Figure.NZ, Ashburton District had 1,605 Filipino people, which is about 4.6% of the district population.

By comparison, the total Asian population in Ashburton was 3,273, meaning Filipinos make up nearly half of the Asian population locally. The same broad ethnic data shows Maori at 3,000, Pacific Peoples at 2,097, Middle Eastern/Latin American/African at 471, and Other ethnicity at 453.
Source: https://figure.nz/chart/kTHySRszQMlknOp2

Also, Figure.NZ language data from Stats NZ shows Ashburton District has a Tagalog-speaking proportion of about 2.17%, compared with approximately 1.77% in Christchurch City, 1.64% in Auckland, and 1.16% in Selwyn. So although Ashburton is much smaller than those centres, proportionally it appears to have one of the strongest Tagalog-speaking fields in New Zealand - HIGHER THAN AUCKLAND AND CHRISTCHURCH.
Source: https://figure.nz/chart/6zVZjwr8WNRNyJ9x

So while it is true that there are many nationalities in the territory, the available census data suggests the Filipino/Tagalog field is somewhat distinct in Ashburton because of its size and concentration. It may not be simply one small nationality among many, but one of the more significant non-English-speaking fields in our local territory.

That also seems consistent with previous evaluations of the group. In the 2018 circuit overseer report, the comment was made: "The spirit of the group is excellent. There is a real need for this language group in the Ashburton."

The same report also noted that "the response of the Filipino people in the ministry is excellent" and that there was "much more potential to grow the number of Bible Studies being conducted." Later, in the 2021 Branch evaluation, the Branch noted that among the "significant Tagalog-speaking population" in the area, there were interested persons "studying the Bible and making good progress toward becoming disciples," and approved the recommendation that the group continue to function.

I also understand the point that many Filipinos have chosen to come to an English-speaking country and therefore need to learn English. That is true in a practical sense, and many do function well in English for work and daily life. However, I wonder if the main question for us is not only whether they can function in English, but whether the arrangement helps them understand the truth accurately, progress spiritually, and feel shepherded.

That seems to harmonize with the direction in Shepherd, Chapter 16, "Multilanguage Fields." It states that Jehovah wants "all sorts of people" to hear the Kingdom message and become disciples, and that the purpose of preaching and teaching in another language is to assist interested individuals and publishers who are dependent on that language. It also clarifies that the purpose is not to promote a certain language or culture, but to help those who need that language to understand the truth accurately and progress spiritually.

The same chapter encourages elders to know well the needs in the congregation's territory and congregation, including how many interested individuals and publishers are dependent on the language. That principle seems especially relevant here, because the local numbers indicate that this is a sizeable field in our assigned territory.

I was also reminded of the general shepherding principles in Shepherd, Chapter 2. Elders are encouraged to take a loving and active interest in the spiritual, emotional, and physical needs of others, and the objective of shepherding is to impart something faith-strengthening and encouraging. For some publishers and interested ones, especially newer migrants, families, children, or those still adjusting to life in New Zealand, spiritual encouragement in their heart language may have a meaningful effect on their progress and stability.

Of course, I fully appreciate that this needs to be balanced with what the elder body can realistically handle. I am not opposed to simplification where needed. At the same time, I noticed that the 2018 circuit overseer report described search work as "an essential part of the ministry for foreign language congregations and groups" and said that it "needs to be a permanent part of their territory." So while updating the list and caring for the territory is real work, it may be part of the normal responsibility of caring for a foreign-language field rather than, by itself, a reason to discontinue the arrangement.

The same report also suggested that one key to the group's progress was for the host body of elders to "take an active interest in developing the target-language field." That makes me wonder whether the better question may be how we can support the arrangement more sustainably, rather than whether the need still exists.

My main thought is simply that before we consider discontinuing the group, it may be worth asking whether the Tagalog field represents a unique local spiritual opportunity that Jehovah has placed in our territory. If there was a need when the group was formed, the available data and previous evaluations seem to suggest that the need has not diminished; if anything, the Filipino/Tagalog-speaking presence in Ashburton appears significant and continuing.

Thank you again, brothers, for opening the discussion in such a balanced way. I am happy to support whatever direction the body prayerfully decides, but I felt these points may be useful to consider as part of the discussion.

Warm Christian love,
Adam

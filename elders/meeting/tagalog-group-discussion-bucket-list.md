# Tagalog Group Discussion - Bucket List

Working notes for framing the discussion about the Ashburton Tagalog group.

## Meeting Agenda Context

The meeting agenda is for Ashburton Congregation, dated 19/06/26, 6:30pm at the Kingdom Hall. The PDF filename says 19_07_26, but the agenda itself says 19/06/26.

The Tagalog group item is framed this way: "How are the current arrangements going? Please be prepared to discuss the needs of the congregation and the Tagalog group." This is important because the agenda is not simply asking whether the group should continue or stop. It asks for a discussion of both the congregation's needs and the Tagalog group's needs.

The surrounding agenda items are also relevant. The body will discuss BOE communication, regular encouraging shepherding visits, publishers needing support, and training. That means the best framing should connect the Tagalog discussion with the wider themes of shepherding, workload, communication, and training, rather than treating it as a separate yes/no question.

Useful framing from the agenda:

> "Since the agenda asks us to discuss the needs of both the congregation and the Tagalog group, perhaps we could separate the question into three parts: what spiritual need exists in the Tagalog field, what support the congregation needs from the elders, and what arrangement would care for both without overloading the body."

> "The agenda wording helps me because it does not make this only a question of whether the group continues. It asks how the current arrangements are going and what the needs are. That suggests we first need a clear picture of spiritual need, workload, shepherding impact, and possible adjustments."

## Core Frame

- The aim is not to preserve a language arrangement for its own sake.
- The main question is whether there are publishers, Bible students, return visits, or interested persons in our assigned territory who need Tagalog to understand the truth accurately and progress spiritually.
- Before discontinuing the group, it would be good to establish whether the spiritual need still exists and whether there is a sustainable way to care for that need.
- The discussion can be framed as: "I am not saying the group must continue exactly as it is. I am asking that before we discontinue it, we first establish whether the spiritual need still exists, who is actually dependent on Tagalog, and whether there is a sustainable way to care for that need."

## Tone to Maintain

- Cooperative, not oppositional.
- Respectful of the elder body's workload and the need for simplification.
- Focused on spiritual need, not culture, nationality, or personal preference.
- Willing to support whatever the body prayerfully decides.
- Avoid sounding like the issue is already settled.

## Strong Points Already Raised

- 2023 Census data summarized by Figure.NZ shows Ashburton District had 1,605 Filipino people, about 4.6% of the district population.
- The total Asian population in Ashburton was 3,273, so Filipinos make up nearly half of the Asian population locally.
- Other broad ethnic figures from the same data include Maori at 3,000, Pacific Peoples at 2,097, Middle Eastern/Latin American/African at 471, and Other ethnicity at 453.
- Figure.NZ language data from Stats NZ shows Ashburton District has a Tagalog-speaking proportion of about 2.17%.
- This compares with approximately 1.77% in Christchurch City, 1.64% in Auckland, and 1.16% in Selwyn.
- Better wording than "higher than Auckland and Christchurch": "Proportionally, Ashburton appears to have a notably concentrated Tagalog-speaking field, even compared with larger centres."
- The Filipino/Tagalog field appears to be distinct locally because of its size and concentration.
- The field may not be simply one small nationality among many, but one of the more significant non-English-speaking fields in our territory.

## Previous Evaluations to Mention

- 2018 circuit overseer report: "The spirit of the group is excellent. There is a real need for this language group in the Ashburton."
- 2018 report also noted that "the response of the Filipino people in the ministry is excellent."
- 2018 report referred to "much more potential to grow the number of Bible Studies being conducted."
- 2021 Branch evaluation referred to a "significant Tagalog-speaking population" in the area.
- 2021 Branch evaluation noted interested persons "studying the Bible and making good progress toward becoming disciples."
- 2021 Branch approved the recommendation that the group continue to function.

## Important Distinction

- Filipino ethnicity or Tagalog ability does not automatically mean a person is dependent on Tagalog for spiritual progress.
- The stronger question is: Who actually depends on Tagalog, or is meaningfully helped by Tagalog, to understand Bible truth and make spiritual progress?
- The local census data supports the existence of a sizeable field, but the elder body still needs local spiritual facts.

## Questions Worth Asking

- How many publishers currently depend on Tagalog for spiritual food?
- How many Bible students or progressive interested ones depend on or strongly prefer Tagalog?
- How many interested persons from the territory have attended Tagalog meetings or accepted ongoing help in Tagalog?
- Are there newer migrants, families, children, or older ones who function in English practically but benefit spiritually from Tagalog?
- What has the ministry response been like recently?
- Has search work been done consistently enough to evaluate the field accurately?
- If the current format is too much, what smaller arrangement could still care for the need?
- Would a reduced schedule, periodic meetings, targeted search work, or shared support with nearby congregations be practical?
- Could we trial a sustainable version before deciding to discontinue the group?

## Possible Meeting Wording

"I agree we need to be realistic about elder capacity and not maintain an arrangement just because it has existed before."

"The question may not be, 'Do Filipinos live here?' but, 'Are there publishers or interested ones who need Tagalog to understand the truth accurately and progress spiritually?'"

"Could we first identify how many publishers, Bible students, return visits, or interested persons are actually dependent on or meaningfully helped by Tagalog?"

"If the current format is too heavy, could we consider a reduced arrangement, less frequent meetings, targeted search work, shared support with nearby congregations, or a trial period before discontinuing?"

"I will support the body's decision. I just feel the decision will be stronger if we can show that we weighed the local spiritual need and not only the workload."

"My main concern is not preserving a language arrangement for its own sake. It is making sure that if there are people in our assigned territory who still need Tagalog to progress spiritually, we have prayerfully considered how they will be cared for before deciding to discontinue the group."

## Sustainable Alternatives to Explore

- Continue the group as currently arranged.
- Continue the group with a reduced meeting schedule.
- Hold only selected meeting portions in Tagalog.
- Arrange periodic Tagalog public talks or Watchtower Studies.
- Strengthen targeted search work rather than broad, time-heavy search work.
- Coordinate support from nearby Tagalog-speaking publishers or congregations if available.
- Use videoconference support where appropriate.
- Maintain a local care list of Tagalog-speaking interested ones and publishers needing support.
- Set a trial period with clear review points.
- Define what evidence would show that the group should continue, reduce, pause, or discontinue.

## Risks to Avoid

- Making the argument sound cultural rather than spiritual.
- Treating census ethnicity as proof of language dependence.
- Appearing to minimize the elder body's workload.
- Sounding like the Branch or circuit overseer reports settle the present question by themselves.
- Overstating the data with wording that sounds argumentative.
- Letting the discussion become about personal attachment to the group rather than the needs of people in the territory.

## Evidence Still Needed

- Current number of Tagalog publishers, Bible students, and interested persons.
- Current number of those who are dependent on Tagalog.
- Meeting attendance trends.
- Ministry response from recent search work.
- Current workload required to maintain the arrangement.
- Available qualified brothers and publishers who can support the arrangement.
- Nearby congregations or resources that could help.
- Whether any individuals would likely be spiritually weakened if the arrangement ended.

## Additional Brother Feedback

### Richard

Richard sees this as a sensitive subject and strongly acknowledges the good work done by the Tagalog brothers in finding, fostering, and supporting significant interest in the territory. He also makes the point that, regardless of what the body decides about the group, continued effort in the Filipino/Tagalog field will still be needed. Bible studies would continue in the mother tongue where needed.

His main caution is around the word "dependent." He notes that Tagalog is not necessarily the first language of all Filipino brothers and sisters, since many have another dialect. He also reasons that because basic English is required to live in New Zealand under visa arrangements, that may weaken the claim that ones are dependent on Tagalog.

Richard also acknowledges Adamson's point that the host body needs to take an active interest in the group. He suggests that English brothers attending the Saturday meeting could help, but feels this would likely be more successful if there was only one meeting. He also highlights that the Filipino brothers, sisters, and children bring warmth and energy to the English congregation, and that the whole congregation benefits from their presence. He feels those directly caring for the group should speak for themselves about the energy output involved.

### Ross

Ross appreciates hearing both the reasons for continuing the group and the reasons that make it challenging. He frames the issue around one simple question: Can the body continue to support the group?

His main concern is shepherding. He notes that shepherding is becoming more and more important in the last days, yet when field service groups were reorganized, some elders said they could not serve as group overseer or assistant. The congregation has already reduced the number of groups by one and now has 9 groups, which he feels is not ideal.

Ross is also concerned about burnout and medical support. He observes that when brothers or sisters need medical assistance, it often falls to the older elders. His questions are whether the body is comfortable sacrificing shepherding time to support the group, and whether the body is comfortable leaving medical matters mostly with the older elders. He presents simplification as a possible way to ensure shepherding and medical needs are properly cared for, while making clear that he is not trying to force the matter one way or the other.

### Tony

Tony agrees that it is good to review past decisions from time to time because circumstances change. His view is that the body should continue supporting the Tagalog group. His reason is that, over the 10 years he has been in Ashburton, the vast majority of interest in the territory has come from the Filipino community, both numerically and proportionally. If having meetings in their language helps them progress spiritually, he feels the body should rejoice and foster that growth.

At the same time, Tony does not support excessive measures, such as completely altering the English congregation schedule or meeting times. He feels that would be counterproductive and unnecessary.

Tony strongly agrees that shepherding needs priority, especially as we get deeper into the last days. However, he is unclear how shepherding is being impacted by support for the Tagalog group. His question is whether support for the Tagalog group and shepherding are truly mutually exclusive.

On medical matters, Tony explains that because he lives opposite the hospital and has been used as a contact in the past, brothers and sisters sometimes naturally come to him. He is happy to help where he can. However, he feels that when a scheduled surgical procedure is known about, the group overseer should normally care for the preliminaries, such as helping the publisher review relevant material and ensuring the medical card is up to date. He is still willing to assist with HLC contact, especially at short notice.

Tony notes that younger elders may find medical matters challenging, just as he did when younger. But with age and experience, these situations can become a beautiful privilege of service. He supports simplification in principle, though he is unsure how to apply it in today's complicated world.

## Pushback Map

### Worth Pushing Back

- The idea that basic English ability for visas voids dependence on Tagalog.
- The assumption that shepherding time is necessarily being sacrificed because of the Tagalog group.
- The framing that the only options are current arrangement or discontinuing the group.
- The idea that one combined meeting would automatically solve support, warmth, or workload issues.
- Any decision made without identifying actual dependents, interested ones, studies, meeting attendance, ministry response, and workload.

### Push Back Carefully

- Tagalog not being the first dialect for every Filipino.
- The benefit of Filipino brothers and sisters to the English congregation.
- Medical support falling mainly to older elders.
- Elder burnout and group overseer workload.

### Do Not Push Back

- The need for simplification where appropriate.
- The need to prioritize shepherding.
- The need to avoid burning out brothers.
- The need to apply the direction on "dependent" accurately.
- The value Filipino brothers and sisters bring to the whole congregation.
- The need for those directly carrying the group workload to speak honestly.

## Suggested Pushback Wording

### On English Ability and Dependence

"I agree that the direction uses the word 'dependent,' and we should be careful not to stretch that. At the same time, I wonder if basic English for work or visa purposes is the same thing as understanding spiritual truth accurately and progressing to spiritual maturity. A person may manage daily life in English but still be significantly helped spiritually in Tagalog. Could we assess that from the actual publishers, students, and interested ones rather than assume it from immigration requirements?"

### On Dialects

"That is a fair point that Tagalog is not the first language of every Filipino. Perhaps the question is not whether Tagalog is everyone's native dialect, but whether it is the shared language that best helps the greatest number in that field understand spiritual things. We may need local evidence on that."

### On Shepherding Versus the Group

"I fully agree that shepherding has to be protected. My only hesitation is whether we have established that support for the Tagalog group is what is preventing shepherding from being done. If the issue is overall workload, maybe we need to identify the actual pressure points before concluding that the group itself is the main cause."

### On Sacrificing Shepherding Time

"I would not be comfortable sacrificing shepherding. But I also would not want to frame it as shepherding versus the Tagalog field unless that is clearly what is happening. Could we list what time the group actually requires from the body, which brothers are carrying that load, and whether there is a lighter arrangement that preserves both shepherding and the language field?"

### On Medical Matters Falling to Older Elders

"That concern seems valid, but I am not sure it is directly caused by the Tagalog group. It may show that we need a clearer arrangement for medical support and training younger elders, regardless of what happens with the group."

### On One Meeting

"I can see the benefit of more association together and the warmth the Filipino friends bring to the congregation. At the same time, if some are spiritually helped by Tagalog, one combined meeting may solve one problem while creating another. Could we look at whether there is a way to increase association without removing the Tagalog spiritual support?"

### On Simplification

"I support simplification if it helps us care better for the congregation. I wonder if the first step is to define what exactly needs simplifying: the meeting schedule, preparation load, search work, oversight, medical support, or field service group responsibilities. Once we identify that, we may find a solution that is more precise than simply discontinuing the group."

### On Decision Process

"Before we decide, could we gather the local facts around three areas: spiritual need, elder workload, and possible lighter arrangements? That would help us make a decision that is both compassionate and sustainable."

## Verbatim Pushback Guidance

What to push back:

The main pushback should be on this idea:

> "Basic English needed for NZ life means they are not dependent on Tagalog."

That is the weakest argument. You can say:

> "I agree we need to apply 'dependent' carefully. But I wonder if basic English for work, visas, or daily life is the same as understanding spiritual truth accurately and progressing to maturity. Someone may function in English practically but still be significantly helped spiritually in Tagalog. Could we assess that from the actual publishers, students, and interested ones rather than assume it from immigration requirements?"

Second, push back on Ross's implied framing:

> "Are we sacrificing shepherding time to support the group?"

Say:

> "I would not be comfortable sacrificing shepherding. But I am not sure we have established that support for the Tagalog group is what is preventing shepherding. Could we identify the actual pressure points first: meeting preparation, search work, oversight, field service groups, medical matters, or general elder workload?"

Third, push back on "simplification = discontinue":

> "I support simplification. I just wonder if the first step is to define what exactly needs simplifying. It may be the schedule, the preparation load, search work, or medical support arrangements. Once we identify that, we may find a more precise solution than discontinuing the group."

Be careful not to push back against the valid concerns. Agree strongly with shepherding, burnout, medical care, and the need to apply "dependent" accurately. Your strongest position is:

> "Let's not decide from assumptions. Let's gather local facts in three areas: spiritual need, elder workload, and possible lighter arrangements."

That keeps you balanced and makes the case harder to dismiss.

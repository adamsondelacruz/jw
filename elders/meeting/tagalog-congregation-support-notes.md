# Tagalog Congregation - Support Notes

Working notes for supporting the possible formation of a Tagalog congregation in Ashburton.

## Main Frame

The question is not simply whether we have enough people who speak Tagalog, or whether Filipino brothers and sisters can function in English. The stronger question is whether there are publishers, Bible students, and interested ones who are dependent on Tagalog, or significantly helped by Tagalog, in order to understand the truth accurately, receive meaningful shepherding, express themselves freely, and progress to spiritual maturity.

That is the spiritual issue. If that need exists, then the body should not view the language arrangement merely as an extra workload or a cultural preference. It may be a means of caring for a real spiritual need in the territory.

## Suggested Way to Say It

Brothers, I appreciate the need to be realistic about the responsibility involved. Forming a congregation is not a small step, and it would require the Tagalog elders and ministerial servants to carry real congregational responsibilities.

At the same time, I wonder if we should first identify what Jehovah may already have placed in front of us. We appear to have a sizeable local Tagalog-speaking field, 30 publishers, 5 elders, and 2 ministerial servants. That is not a weak foundation. In fact, compared with the examples in S-50 of situations where a congregation should not be recommended, our situation appears considerably stronger.

The key point, as I understand it, is not whether ones can use English in daily life. Many can. The question is whether they need Tagalog, or are significantly helped by Tagalog, to understand the truth accurately, receive shepherding in a meaningful way, and progress spiritually. From what we have seen in the field, in the group, and from previous evaluations, there do appear to be publishers and interested ones for whom Tagalog is a real spiritual help, not simply a cultural preference.

So perhaps the question is not only, "Can we handle this?" but also, "Has Jehovah already provided enough to care for this field, and are we willing to organize ourselves to support it?"

## S-50 Points That Appear Strong

S-50 is direction for recommending a new congregation. It gives a higher standard than simply continuing as a group. Even so, several points appear strong in our situation.

There is a significant local field. The 2023 Census data summarized by Figure.NZ shows Ashburton District had 1,605 Filipino people, about 4.6% of the district population. The total Asian population in Ashburton was 3,273, meaning Filipinos make up nearly half of the Asian population locally. Figure.NZ language data also shows Ashburton District has about 2.17% Tagalog speakers, proportionally higher than Christchurch City, Auckland, and Selwyn in the comparison previously noted.

There is a significant number of publishers. We have 30 publishers connected with the Tagalog arrangement. That is a substantial base for a language field, especially in a district the size of Ashburton.

There is a strong appointed foundation. We have 5 elders and 2 ministerial servants. S-50 gives examples where a new congregation should not be recommended because there is only one appointed man or an insufficient number of appointed men. That does not appear to describe our situation.

There appears to be a solid core of mature publishers who are fluent in the language. The group has functioned for years, has had previous positive evaluations, and has continued to support meetings, ministry, and interested ones.

Previous evaluations support that the need is real. The 2018 circuit overseer report said there was a real need for the language group in Ashburton, that the spirit of the group was excellent, that the response of the Filipino people in the ministry was excellent, and that there was more potential for Bible studies. The 2021 Branch evaluation referred to a significant Tagalog-speaking population and noted interested ones studying the Bible and progressing toward becoming disciples.

## Key Point on "Dependent"

The word "dependent" should be understood spiritually, not merely practically.

A person may be able to work, shop, study, and communicate in basic English, while still needing Tagalog to understand spiritual matters deeply, express what is in the heart, respond to shepherding, and progress to maturity. Basic English ability does not automatically remove spiritual language dependence.

Suggested wording:

> I agree that we must apply the word "dependent" carefully. But I wonder if we should understand dependence in the spiritual sense. A person may function in English for work or daily life, yet still need Tagalog to understand the truth accurately, express themselves freely, receive meaningful shepherding, and progress spiritually. So I would be cautious about concluding that basic English ability means there is no language dependence.

## Answering Ross's Concern

Ross's concern is valid. Forming a congregation would mean real responsibility. The Tagalog elders would need to be prepared to care for assignments, shepherding, meeting parts, field service, records, communication, and other congregation needs. Calving season, work pressure, family responsibilities, and Bethel assignments are real factors.

At the same time, those concerns do not necessarily mean the congregation should not be formed. They mean the brothers need to count the cost honestly and organize the workload clearly.

Suggested wording:

> I agree with Ross that this should not be approached lightly. If a congregation is formed, the Tagalog elders and ministerial servants must be willing to carry the responsibility, not assume that the English body will continue to carry much of the load. That said, the fact that responsibility is involved does not by itself mean the step is unwise. Every congregation requires faith, willingness, training, and organization. The question is whether the spiritual need and the available brothers indicate that Jehovah has provided enough to move forward.

Another possible wording:

> Perhaps we could separate two questions. First, does the Tagalog field meet the spiritual need and S-50 considerations for a congregation? Second, if it does, what practical arrangement would allow the elders and servants to carry the load sustainably? If we combine those two too quickly, we may allow workload concerns to settle a spiritual question before we have fully assessed it.

## Answering the Concern About English Brothers' Limited Support

It is realistic that the English brothers may only be able to provide limited assistance if a Tagalog congregation is formed. They will be caring for the English congregation without the regular support of the Tagalog brothers.

However, this may actually support the case for clarity. If the Tagalog field is spiritually distinct and has 30 publishers, 5 elders, and 2 ministerial servants, then forming a congregation may allow each body to focus more clearly on its own congregation's needs.

Suggested wording:

> It is true that the English brothers may have limited capacity to assist once the congregation is formed. But that also raises another point: if the Tagalog field has its own publishers, elders, servants, meetings, ministry, and shepherding needs, then a separate congregation may actually make responsibilities clearer. Instead of one body trying to carry two language needs, each body could focus on the sheep assigned to its care.

## Using Dan's Thought Carefully

Dan's thought is helpful because it balances practical planning with faith. The David census account should be used carefully. We do not want to suggest that counting publishers, elders, or workload is wrong. S-50 itself requires careful counting and evaluation.

The point is better framed this way: We should count the cost, but not let visible limitations become the only factor.

Suggested wording:

> I appreciated Dan's thought. Of course, S-50 requires us to look carefully at the practical facts, so we do need to count publishers, appointed men, meeting capacity, and workload. But we also do not want to reason only from what feels manageable from a human standpoint. If the need is real and Jehovah has provided willing brothers, we should be careful not to let fear of responsibility outweigh the opportunity to care for the field.

## Nehemiah Illustration

The account of Nehemiah is also useful. When Jehovah's people were rebuilding the wall, the work was not easy and there was opposition. Yet they did not stop the work simply because it became demanding. They organized themselves so that the work could continue while still protecting the people. Nehemiah 4:17, 18 describes the builders working with one hand while the other hand held a weapon, and each builder had his sword at his hip.

The point is not that we should take on work recklessly or ignore practical limits. The point is that when Jehovah's people see an important work that needs to be done, they prayerfully organize themselves, accept the responsibility, and answer the call.

Suggested wording:

> I was also thinking of Nehemiah. When the wall was being rebuilt, the work was difficult and there was pressure from opposers. But Jehovah's people did not conclude that the work should stop just because it required more effort. They organized themselves so the work could continue while the people were protected. In a similar way, if this Tagalog field is a real spiritual need that Jehovah has placed before us, perhaps the question is not whether it will be easy, but whether we can organize ourselves faithfully and practically to care for it.

## Strong Argument for Formation

If I were to summarize the positive case, I would say:

> The case for forming a Tagalog congregation is not based merely on culture or preference. It rests on several spiritual and practical factors: a sizeable local Tagalog-speaking field, 30 publishers, 5 elders, 2 ministerial servants, previous positive evaluations, evidence of interest in the ministry, and the reality that some publishers and interested ones are spiritually helped by Tagalog. If those ones are dependent on Tagalog, or significantly helped by it, to understand the truth and progress spiritually, then forming a congregation may be a reasonable and faith-strengthening way to care for that need.

## Points We Should Be Ready to Establish

To make the recommendation stronger, we should be ready to answer these questions:

- How many of the 30 publishers are dependent on Tagalog for spiritual food?
- How many Bible students or interested ones are attending or progressing and dependent on or strongly helped by Tagalog?
- Can the local brothers conduct all weekly meetings without depending on JW Stream?
- Are the majority of appointed men fluent enough in Tagalog to provide oversight and meeting support?
- How will shepherding be organized?
- How will assignments be shared during calving season and other heavy work periods?
- What assistance, if any, would be needed from English brothers, and for how long?
- Is the Kingdom Hall arrangement practical?
- Would forming a congregation help clarify responsibilities for both the Tagalog and English fields?

## Balanced Closing Wording

Brothers, I do not want to minimize the responsibility involved. If this goes ahead, the Tagalog elders and ministerial servants would need to step up and carry the load in a real way. But when I look at the field, the number of publishers, the appointed brothers available, the previous evaluations, and the spiritual benefit that Tagalog provides to some, I feel there is a strong basis to consider that Jehovah may have provided what is needed.

Perhaps the question is not whether this will be effortless. It will not be. The question is whether the spiritual need is real, whether the S-50 considerations are substantially met, and whether the brothers are willing to organize themselves to care for the responsibility. If the answer to those questions is yes, then I feel the formation of a Tagalog congregation deserves serious and positive consideration.

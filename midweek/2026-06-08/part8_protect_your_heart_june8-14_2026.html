Part 8 — "Protect Your Heart From Misinformation"

Living as Christians | 8 minutes (conductor) | Week of June 8–14, 2026 (Jeremiah 4-6)
Features video: Protect Yourself From Misinformation (4:52)

Part scriptures (use first): Isaiah 30:10 · Jeremiah 5:31 · Job 12:11
Audience questions (the three in the part — these are the questions):
  1. Why is misinformation dangerous?
  2. What is deepfake technology, and why do we need to be aware of it?
  3. How can we put information to the test? — Job 12:11
Reflective close (ASK YOURSELF): "Do I tend to trust only information that confirms what I want to believe? Or do I keep my biases in check?"
Timing: Intro ~1:15 → Video 4:52 → Discussion + Close ~1:55. Total 8:00.

Introduction (~1:15)

(warm, conversational) Brothers and sisters, we live in a time when more information reaches us in a single day than reached our grandparents in a whole year. (pause) But here is the difficult question — how much of it is actually true?

(thoughtful) Most of us have experienced it. We see a story online, we share it, and later we find out it wasn't true at all. Or we hear something repeated so many times that we simply start to believe it.

(serious) Misinformation isn't new. Jehovah saw it in his own people thousands of years ago. Notice what the Israelites told their prophets at Isaiah 30:10:

"Tell us flattering things; envision deceptive illusions."

(pause) They didn't want the truth. They wanted what felt good. And Jeremiah 5:31 — right in this week's Bible reading — adds this heartbreaking detail:

"The prophets prophesy lies... and my own people love it that way."

(reflective) Misinformation succeeds not because it overpowers us — it succeeds because it appeals to us. To our fears. To our preferences. To our pride. (transition) The video we're about to watch will help us see how to protect our hearts. Let's watch.

▶ PLAY VIDEO — Protect Yourself From Misinformation (4:52)

Discussion (~1:30)

(warm) Thank you. Let's consider three questions.

Q1. Why is misinformation dangerous?
(take 1–2 brief comments)
(prime if needed: shapes decisions, damages faith, divides families, undermines trust in Jehovah's organization)
Q2. What is deepfake technology, and why do we need to be aware of it?
(take 1–2 brief comments)
(prime if needed: AI can now fabricate convincing video and voice of real people — we can no longer trust our eyes and ears alone)
Q3. How can we put information to the test? — and notice how Job 12:11 puts it:
"Does not the ear test out words as the palate tastes food?"
(take 1–2 brief comments)
(prime if needed: check the source, use jw.org and JW Library, slow down before sharing, ask a mature brother, consider the motive)

Closing reflection (~25 sec)

(slow, sincere) Then ask yourself — and this one stings a little: (pause) "Do I tend to trust only information that confirms what I want to believe? Or do I keep my biases in check?"

(conclude, warm) Remember — our feeds and social media are tailored to our likes, so we often need to dig deeper. (slow) Jeremiah's people didn't fall because they couldn't see lies. They fell because they loved the lies. (pause) Test what you hear — and protect your heart.

Delivery notes

  • Total: 8:00. Video 4:52. Conductor speaking ≈ 3:00 (intro 1:15 + Q&A 1:30 + close 0:25)
  • Scriptures are the part's own: Isa 30:10, Jer 5:31, Job 12:11
  • The three audience questions are non-negotiable — do not add a fourth
  • Cap each audience comment at ~20 sec; if running long, drop priming and move to next Q
  • Tone is direct and serious; lightness is minimal by design — only the closing "this one stings a little" softens the reflection
  • The closing echoes Jeremiah 5:31 ("they loved the lies") to bookend the part

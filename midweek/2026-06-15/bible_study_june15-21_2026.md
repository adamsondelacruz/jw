# Lesson 94 — Disciples Receive Holy Spirit at Pentecost

## Comment 94-1 — They were *together*

> *"Now while the day of the festival of Pentecost was being celebrated, they were all **together** in the same place."* — Acts 2:1

**Comment (~26 sec):**

What I notice in Acts 2:1 is that *before* the holy spirit came, the disciples were *together*. All in the same place. Waiting. Praying. They didn't scatter — they gathered. It tells me Jehovah pours out his spirit on those who choose unity over isolation. That's why being at the meeting, being with the brothers, matters more than we sometimes realize.

---

## Comment 94-2 — Heard in his own language

> *"Each one heard them speaking in his **own language**."* — Acts 2:6

**Comment (~28 sec):**

What struck me about Acts 2:6 is the direction of the miracle. Jehovah didn't expect the crowd to learn the disciples' language. He gave the disciples the *crowd's* language. That's how Jehovah works — he meets people where they are. In the ministry, that's our pattern too: we learn the language, the culture, the heart of the person in front of us.

---

## Comment 94-3 — Peter, transformed

> *"But Peter **stood up** with the eleven and raised his voice and made this declaration to them..."* — Acts 2:14

**Comment (~25 sec):**

Just seven weeks earlier, Peter had denied Jesus three times — afraid of a servant girl. In Acts 2:14, that same Peter stands up in front of thousands and preaches Christ. What changed? Holy spirit. It tells me Jehovah's spirit doesn't just help us — it *transforms* us. The shyest publisher today could be the bold preacher of tomorrow.

---

## Comment 94-4 — Day after day

> *"They **continued devoting** themselves to the teaching of the apostles, to associating together, to the taking of meals, and to prayers."* — Acts 2:42

**Comment (~28 sec):**

Acts 2:42 is a beautiful snapshot of the early congregation. Four habits — teaching, fellowship, meals together, prayer. Notice the words *"continued devoting themselves."* It wasn't a Pentecost high that faded. It was a daily routine. It reminds me that what makes a strong Christian isn't the dramatic moment. It's the daily one. The same four habits, today, still hold us up.

# Lesson 95 — Could Not Stop the Disciples From Preaching

## Comment 95-1 — Silver and gold I do not have

> *"**Silver and gold I do not have**, but what I do have I give you: In the name of Jesus Christ the Nazarene, walk!"* — Acts 3:6

**Comment (~29 sec):**

What moved me about Acts 3:6 is Peter's honesty. The lame man asked for money. Peter said, *"Silver and gold I don't have."* But he didn't stop there — *"What I do have I give you."* In the ministry, we sometimes feel inadequate because we can't fix every problem in a person's life. But we *do* have something better. We have the truth about Jehovah's Kingdom.

---

## Comment 95-2 — Recognized as having "been with Jesus"

> *"They began to recognize about them that they **used to be with Jesus**."* — Acts 4:13

**Comment (~28 sec):**

In Acts 4:13, the religious leaders were astonished — these were unlettered, ordinary men. But they noticed one thing about them: *they had been with Jesus.* You could see it in them. That's a powerful question for me. When people meet me — at work, on the bus, at the door — do they see someone who has been with Jesus? Or just someone with a religion?

---

## Comment 95-3 — Obey God as ruler

> *"We must **obey God as ruler** rather than men."* — Acts 5:29

**Comment (~29 sec):**

When Peter said in Acts 5:29, *"We must obey God as ruler rather than men,"* he wasn't being defiant for its own sake. He was being clear about who came first. What I appreciate about Jehovah is that he doesn't ask for *part* of our loyalty. He asks for the throne. And when *that* is settled in our heart, every other authority falls into its proper place.

---

## Comment 95-4 — Rejoicing at being dishonored

> *"...they went their way... **rejoicing** because they had been counted worthy to be dishonored in behalf of his name."* — Acts 5:41

**Comment (~26 sec):**

What gets me about Acts 5:41 is the word *rejoicing*. The apostles had just been beaten. They walked out *rejoicing*. Not because suffering is fun — but because they had been counted worthy to suffer for Jesus' name. It reminds me that the real test of love for Jehovah isn't comfort. It's whether opposition makes me bitter — or grateful.

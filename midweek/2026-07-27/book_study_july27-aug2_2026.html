Walk Courageously With God (wcg) | Intro to Section 1, Timeline, and Chapter 1

Theme to keep returning to: Courage is not just a personality trait. Jehovah can help ordinary humans walk with him, stand apart from the world, and speak when it is hard.

Opening Comments (~1 min)

Brothers and sisters, tonight we begin the first full study from our new book, Walk Courageously With God. The section we are starting covers a very long period of Bible history, from the days of the patriarchs down to the time of the judges.

But the point is not just history. We are looking at real people who had fears, pressure, opposition, and difficult assignments. Yet Jehovah helped them show courage. Tonight we will start with Enoch, a man who lived in a violent and ungodly world but still “walked with the true God.” As we conduct the study, let us keep asking: What kind of courage can Jehovah help me show now?

Section 1 Intro - From the Days of the Patriarchs to the Time of the Judges

Adlib Before Reading / Discussing

This section covers about 22 centuries. That is a massive sweep of time, but the book does not treat it as cold history. It highlights people who had to make courageous choices: Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Moses, Rahab, Deborah, Gideon, and others. Some had to travel, some had to preach, some had to stand alone, and some had to fight “the wars of Jehovah.”

Question 1

Question: Looking at the section intro, what kind of situations required courage from Jehovah’s servants in this period?

Listen for: long journeys, danger, standing apart from others, difficult assignments, fighting Jehovah’s wars, serving Jehovah when the world around them did not.

Commendation hint: “That is a helpful way to group the examples. You showed that courage can appear in different forms, not only in dramatic moments.”

Possible Follow-Up

Follow-up: Why is it encouraging that this section covers such a variety of people and situations?

Direction: It helps everyone find something personal. Courage may be needed in the family, ministry, school, work, health issues, or when we feel alone.

Interesting thought: “I like that point because it brings the section close to us. These are not distant Bible characters; they show courage we may need this week.”

Section 1 Timeline

Timeline Adlib (~1-2 min)

There are a couple of details on the timeline that are worth noticing.

First, Enoch lived while Adam was still alive. Adam lived from 4026 to 3096 B.C.E., and Enoch lived from 3404 to 3039 B.C.E. So Enoch lived during the lifetime of someone who had seen Eden before sin entered the world. Yet Enoch still had to make his own choice to walk with Jehovah. Courage could not be inherited.

Second, the timeline moves from the pre-Flood world, to the Flood in 2370 B.C.E., to Abraham, to the Exodus in 1513 B.C.E., and then to Israel entering Canaan in 1473 B.C.E. That helps us see that Jehovah’s purpose kept moving forward through many generations. Human conditions changed, but Jehovah kept strengthening faithful people to do his will.

Question 2

Question: What does the timeline help us appreciate about Jehovah’s ability to preserve faithful worship through changing times?

Listen for: Jehovah’s purpose continues across generations; faithful individuals can stand out even when surrounded by bad conditions; Jehovah remembers and uses courageous people at the right time.

Commendation hint: “That was well observed. You connected the dates to Jehovah’s purpose, not just to chronology.”

Optional Follow-Up

Follow-up: Why is it meaningful that Enoch could not rely on Adam’s experience but had to walk with God personally?

Direction: Each person must build personal faith. Family background can help, but courage comes from our own relationship with Jehovah.

Possible response: “Good point. That makes the timeline practical because it reminds us that each generation has to choose Jehovah for itself.”

Chapter 1 - Enoch: He Walked With God

Brief Transition

Now we come to the first person considered in the book: Enoch. The chapter shows his courage in two ways: what he refused to do and what he did do.

Opening Discussion Question

Question 3

Question from the book: In what ways did Enoch show courage?

Listen for: He refused to fit in with the violent, ungodly world; he avoided false worship; he acted as Jehovah’s prophet; he delivered a strong judgment message; he kept walking with God even when he may have faced danger.

Commendation hint: “That is a complete answer. You brought out both sides of Enoch’s courage - what he refused and what he actively did for Jehovah.”

Possible Follow-Up

Follow-up: Why might refusing to fit in sometimes require as much courage as speaking out?

Direction: Standing apart can make a person feel alone. It may invite ridicule or pressure, even before we say anything.

Interesting thought: “That is very practical. Sometimes our conduct gives a witness before our words do.”

Dig Deeper

Question 4

Question from the book: 1. How might people have begun “calling on the name of Jehovah”? (Gen. 4:26; w01 9/15 29 par. 3)

Listen for: They may have used Jehovah’s name in a profane or false-religious way; they may have applied God’s name to humans or idols; it was not pure worship.

Commendation hint: “That clarifies the expression well. You helped us see that using Jehovah’s name is not the same as honoring it.”

Possible Follow-Up

Follow-up: What does Enoch’s example teach us about Jehovah’s name?

Direction: We must not merely use Jehovah’s name; we must love it, revere it, and keep our worship clean.

Possible response: “Yes, that keeps the focus on reverence. Enoch stood apart because Jehovah’s name mattered to him.”

Question 5

Question from the book: 2. Enoch was the first faithful human to do what? How was he able to fulfill his assignment? (w01 9/15 31 par. 5)

Listen for: He was the first faithful human prophet. He was able to fulfill his assignment by faith and with Jehovah’s strengthening and protection.

Commendation hint: “That was concise and accurate. You showed that Enoch’s courage came from faith, not from self-confidence.”

Possible Follow-Up

Follow-up: How can that help us when we feel nervous about giving a witness?

Direction: We can rely on Jehovah, pray for holy spirit, and remember that he can strengthen ordinary people to speak.

Interesting thought: “I appreciate that application. It brings Enoch’s assignment right into our ministry.”

Question 6

Question from the book: 3. Even before he started prophesying, why would Enoch have likely known that his courage would be put to the test? (w06 10/1 19 pars. 13-14) A

Listen for: He knew the history of rebellion, violence, and opposition to Jehovah’s way; Cain had murdered Abel; Lamech boasted of violence; Enoch could expect that a judgment message would not be welcomed.

Commendation hint: “That is a thoughtful answer. You used the background to show why Enoch would not have been surprised by opposition.”

Picture A Prompt

Question: Looking at Picture A, what could Enoch have meditated on that would strengthen his courage?

Direction: Eden, Adam and Eve’s rebellion, Abel’s faith, Cain’s violence, and the certainty that Jehovah’s way is right.

Possible response: “Good observation. Meditation on Jehovah’s dealings can make courage stronger before the test arrives.”

Question 7

Question from the book: 4. How could you show that Enoch was not taken to heaven, as some believe? (John 3:13; Heb. 11:5, 13)

Listen for: John 3:13 says no man had ascended into heaven; Hebrews 11:13 says faithful ones, including those mentioned in the chapter, died without receiving the fulfillment of the promises. So Enoch was not taken to heaven; Jehovah transferred him from danger, apparently into death, while preserving him in His memory.

Commendation hint: “That was well reasoned from the scriptures. You let the Bible explain the point clearly.”

Possible Follow-Up

Follow-up: Why is that still a loving outcome for Enoch?

Direction: Jehovah protected him from his enemies and kept him safe in His memory for the resurrection.

Interesting thought: “That is comforting. Jehovah’s protection is not limited to the present moment; his memory is the safest place to be.”

Reflect on the Lessons

Question 8

Question from the book: When might we especially need courage to preach? Based on Enoch’s example, what can help us? B

Listen for: when people oppose us, mock us, seem intimidating, or when we feel alone; Enoch’s example helps us rely on Jehovah, remember the message is from Him, and keep going even if others dislike it.

Commendation hint: “That was very practical. You helped us connect Enoch’s public courage with situations we may face in the ministry.”

Picture B Prompt

Question: Looking at Picture B, why might that brother need courage even before he says anything?

Direction: Workmates may laugh, he may feel exposed, and he may worry about future treatment at work.

Possible response: “Exactly. The fear is real, but Enoch reminds us that Jehovah notices the courage behind even a simple witness.”

Question 9

Question from the book: How does Jehovah feel when we walk courageously with him? (Prov. 27:11; Heb. 11:5)

Listen for: Jehovah’s heart rejoices; he is pleased with those who show faith; our courage gives Jehovah a reply to his taunter.

Commendation hint: “That answer put Jehovah’s feelings at the center. It is moving to remember that our courage can make our Father rejoice.”

Possible Follow-Up

Follow-up: Why can that thought help when no one else notices our courage?

Direction: Jehovah sees it and values it. His approval matters more than human recognition.

Interesting thought: “That is encouraging. Some courageous choices are quiet, but none of them are invisible to Jehovah.”

Question 10

Question from the book: How might you imitate Enoch’s courage in your life?

Listen for: refusing bad influences; speaking about Jehovah when appropriate; keeping worship clean; staying peaceful in a violent world; not letting ridicule silence us.

Commendation hint: “That is a good personal application. You made Enoch’s example something we can actually practice.”

Possible Follow-Up

Follow-up: What is one small act of courage a Christian might show this week?

Direction: speak to a workmate or classmate, decline unclean entertainment, attend meeting despite tiredness, defend Bible standards kindly, or pray before a hard conversation.

Possible response: “That is realistic and useful. Courage often starts with one small faithful choice.”

Meditate on the Bigger Picture

Question 11

Question from the book: What does this account teach me about Jehovah?

Listen for: Jehovah notices faithful individuals; he strengthens his servants; he protects them; he values courage; he remembers those who please him.

Commendation hint: “That is a warm answer. You helped us see Jehovah as personally attentive to one faithful man in a very dark world.”

Question 12

Question from the book: How does this account tie in with Jehovah’s purposes?

Listen for: Jehovah will remove ungodliness; his judgment is certain; he preserves faithful worship; he moves his purpose forward even when most of the world rejects him.

Commendation hint: “That connected the account to the bigger issue well. Enoch’s message shows that Jehovah’s purpose will succeed.”

Question 13

Question from the book: What would I like to ask Enoch in the resurrection?

Listen for: personal reflections: how he stayed strong, what his preaching was like, what Jehovah told him, how he felt standing alone, what gave him courage.

Commendation hint: “That is a thoughtful question to imagine. It makes the resurrection hope feel very personal.”

Optional Closing Follow-Up

Follow-up: What do you most want to imitate about Enoch?

Direction: walking with God daily, refusing bad influence, speaking courageously, pleasing Jehovah, or trusting that Jehovah remembers loyal ones.

Interesting thought: “That is a fitting takeaway. Enoch’s life was brief compared with some in his time, but his faith is still strengthening us today.”

Closing Thought (~30 sec)

Enoch lived in a world that was violent, profane, and ungodly, yet Jehovah saw him as a man who walked with Him. That means courage is possible even when the surroundings are bad and even when we feel alone. This week, let us imitate Enoch by standing apart from what is wrong, speaking when Jehovah gives us opportunity, and remembering that our courage makes Jehovah’s heart rejoice.

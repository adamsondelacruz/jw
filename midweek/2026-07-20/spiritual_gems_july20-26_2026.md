## 1 · Jeremiah 18:2

> *Potter's house*

What I like about Jer 18:2 is that Jehovah did not teach Jeremiah only by giving him information. He sent him to a workshop. An ordinary place became a classroom. That tells me Jehovah knows how to teach in ways that reach the heart. In the ministry, I can help people notice lessons from daily life, not just give them facts.

---

## 2 · Jeremiah 18:3

> *He went down*

Jer 18:3 shows Jeremiah's obedience. Jehovah told him to go to the potter's house, and Jeremiah went. At that point, he did not yet know the full lesson. That is a useful pattern for me. Sometimes Jehovah's direction becomes clearer after I obey, not before. Faith does not always wait until every detail is explained.

---

## 3 · Jeremiah 18:4

> *Reworked into another vessel*

Jer 18:4 is comforting because the clay became spoiled, but the potter did not immediately discard it. He reworked it. That helps me see Jehovah as patient and skilled. When a person is willing to be shaped, a mistake does not have to be the end of the story. Jehovah can still make something useful and beautiful.

---

## 4 · Jeremiah 18:10

> *Does not obey*

Jer 18:10 reminds me that past blessings do not remove the need for present obedience. Jehovah may intend good for a person or a people, but if they stop listening, they can lose what he wanted to give them. That is sobering. My relationship with Jehovah is not maintained by yesterday's faithfulness. I need to keep listening today.

---

## 5 · Jeremiah 18:13

> *Ask among the nations*

In Jer 18:13, Jehovah says, in effect, "Ask around." He invites comparison. That tells me Jehovah is not afraid of honest thinking. His people had done something shocking, and he wanted them to see how unreasonable their course was. In the ministry, we can do something similar by asking thoughtful questions that help people examine where a course is leading.

---

## 6 · Jeremiah 18:14

> *Snow of Lebanon*

Jer 18:14 uses a natural picture: the snow of Lebanon and cold flowing waters. Those things were dependable. But Jehovah's people had become less dependable than creation itself. That makes me think about loyalty. The natural world quietly obeys Jehovah's design. I want my own worship to be just as steady, not something that dries up when pressure comes.

---

## 7 · Jeremiah 18:15

> *Ancient paths*

Jer 18:15 says God's people left the ancient paths and walked on side roads. That is often how spiritual danger starts. Not with a dramatic rejection of Jehovah, but with little alternative paths that feel harmless. This verse helps me ask: Am I staying on the path Jehovah has marked out, or am I getting comfortable with spiritual shortcuts?

---

## 8 · Jeremiah 18:17

> *My back, not my face*

Jer 18:17 is painful. Jehovah says he would show his back, not his face, in the day of disaster. The worst part of judgment was not just the trouble itself. It was losing Jehovah's favorable attention. That teaches me what repentance protects. When I respond to counsel early, I am really protecting the privilege of having Jehovah's face toward me.

---

## 9 · Jeremiah 18:18

> *Against Jeremiah*

Jer 18:18 shows the confidence of Jeremiah's opposers. They assumed they still had priests, wise men, and prophets, so they did not need to listen to Jeremiah. It is a warning. Religious structure can make people feel secure even when they are resisting Jehovah. I never want confidence in arrangements to become a substitute for humility before Jehovah's word.

---

## 10 · Jeremiah 18:19

> *Pay attention to me*

What moves me in Jer 18:19 is that Jeremiah did not start by fighting his opponents directly. He turned to Jehovah: "Pay attention to me." That is a good pattern when we feel misunderstood or attacked. Before we defend ourselves, we can pour the matter out to Jehovah. He hears the whole conversation, including the parts no one else knows.

---

## 11 · Jeremiah 18:20

> *Speak good about them*

Jer 18:20 shows Jeremiah's conscience. The people were digging a pit for him, but he could say that he had stood before Jehovah to speak good about them. That is real shepherding spirit. In the ministry, and in the congregation, I want to be able to say the same: even when people are difficult, I tried to speak for their good.

---

## 12 · Jeremiah 18:23

> *You know*

Jer 18:23 contains a short but strengthening thought: Jehovah knew all their schemes. Jeremiah did not have to prove every motive or expose every plot by himself. Jehovah already knew. That can calm us when something is unfair. We do not need to carry the burden of seeing everything. Jehovah sees, remembers, and acts at the right time.

---

## 13 · Jeremiah 19:1, 2

> *Valley of the Son of Hinnom*

Jer 19:1, 2 shows that Jeremiah had to carry Jehovah's message to a very uncomfortable place, the Valley of the Son of Hinnom. That was not a pleasant setting for a spiritual message. But Jehovah chose it because the location itself exposed the seriousness of Judah's sin. It reminds me that courage sometimes means speaking the truth where the setting is hard.

---

## 14 · Jeremiah 19:4

> *Made this place unrecognizable*

Jer 19:4 says the people had made that place unrecognizable. That phrase struck me. False worship did not just add a few bad practices; it changed the character of the place. Sin can do that to a person too. It can make someone almost unrecognizable spiritually. But the warning itself shows Jehovah still wanted people to wake up.

---

## 15 · Jeremiah 19:5

> *Never came up in my heart*

Jer 19:5 reveals something beautiful about Jehovah. The people were committing horrible acts in worship of Baal, but Jehovah says such cruelty had never come up in his heart. That tells me he is completely separate from the violence and abuse often connected with false religion. In the ministry, that is worth sharing. Jehovah is not cruel. He is clean.

---

## 📚 R1 · Jeremiah 18:1-4 — *Insight book connection*

> *The potter's house*

The *Insight* material on Jeremiah notes that Jehovah had Jeremiah use dramatic illustrations. The visit to the potter was one of them. That teaches me something about Jehovah as a teacher. He did not give Jeremiah an abstract lecture. He gave him a scene he could watch, remember, and retell. Good teaching often helps people see the point, not just hear it.

---

## 📚 R2 · Jeremiah 19:1, 10, 11 — *Insight book connection*

> *The broken flask*

The *Insight* material also mentions Jeremiah breaking a flask before the older men as a symbol of what would happen to Jerusalem. That must have been unforgettable: the sound, the fragments, the finality. Jehovah's warning was not vague. He made the danger visible. It reminds me that Bible illustrations are not decoration. They can make Jehovah's message land in the conscience.

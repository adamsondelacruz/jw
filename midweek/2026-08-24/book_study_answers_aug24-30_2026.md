# Congregation Bible Study Answers — Week of August 24-30, 2026

**Walk Courageously With God (`wcg`) | Chapter 5: Abraham - He Faced His Hardest Challenge**

Each answer is written as a meeting comment, about 20-30 seconds when spoken. The focus is on giving usable answers, not conducting notes.

---

## Opening Discussion

### 1. In what ways did Abraham show courage during this part of his life?

**Answer option 1:** Abraham showed courage by obeying Jehovah even when the command must have been extremely painful and hard to understand. Isaac was the son he loved and the son connected with Jehovah's promise. Yet Abraham did not delay or argue. He trusted that Jehovah was just, loving, and able to fulfill his promise even if Abraham could not see how.

**Answer option 2:** What impresses me is that Abraham's courage was built on what he had already learned about Jehovah. He had seen Jehovah's justice with Sodom and Gomorrah, and he had seen Jehovah give life when Isaac was born. So when the hardest test came, Abraham did not rely on blind emotion. He relied on a history of trust.

---

## Dig Deeper

### 2. What evidence is there that Abraham was a historical person? (g 5/12 18, box; it "Abraham" ¶22-23) A

**Answer option 1:** There is evidence that the Bible's setting for Abraham is historically reliable. Ancient records mention names similar to those of Abraham's relatives, and archaeology confirms places connected with his life, such as Ur, Haran, and Hebron. Also, Jesus and his disciples referred to Abraham as a real person, not as a legend.

**Answer option 2:** This strengthens my confidence because Abraham is central to Jehovah's purpose. If he were only symbolic, many Bible teachings would lose their historical foundation. But the Bible presents him as real, and historical evidence supports the world he lived in. That helps me trust the record and the promises connected with him.

---

### 3. How might Abraham have learned about Jehovah God? (ia 26 ¶4-5)

**Answer option 1:** Abraham may have learned about Jehovah through the faithful line of his ancestors and through preserved knowledge from earlier generations. He lived close enough in time to people who could pass down reliable information about creation, Eden, the Flood, and Jehovah's dealings with humans. But Abraham still had to make that knowledge personal.

**Answer option 2:** I like the lesson that Abraham did not simply absorb the religion around him. He lived in a world full of false worship, but he chose to know Jehovah. That teaches me that spiritual background can help, but each person has to build personal faith and choose Jehovah for himself.

---

### 4. What made Abraham's worship acceptable to Jehovah? (rr 20 ¶18)

**Answer option 1:** Abraham's worship was acceptable because it was based on faith, obedience, and exclusive devotion to Jehovah. He did not merely perform religious actions. He listened when Jehovah spoke, left his old surroundings, built altars to Jehovah, and showed by his choices that Jehovah was the true God in his life.

**Answer option 2:** This teaches me that acceptable worship is more than believing that Jehovah exists. Abraham's faith moved him to action. He obeyed, sacrificed, and kept trusting Jehovah's promises. So if I want my worship to please Jehovah, it has to affect my decisions, priorities, and willingness to obey.

---

### 5. How does Jehovah's promise to Abraham at Genesis 22:17 confirm the Bible's scientific accuracy? (g88 4/8 25) B

**Answer option 1:** Jehovah compared Abraham's offspring to the stars of the heavens and the grains of sand on the seashore. Both are beyond human counting. That comparison fits what we now know: the number of stars is enormous, far beyond what the unaided eye can count. The Bible used accurate language long before modern astronomy.

**Answer option 2:** This strengthens my appreciation for Jehovah as the Author of the Bible. He could use a simple illustration that Abraham understood, but one that also remains scientifically sound. Jehovah did not need modern instruments to know the vastness of the stars. The Creator knows his creation perfectly.

---

## Reflect on the Lessons

### 6. How did Abraham's hope help him to be courageous? How will focusing on our hope help us to be courageous? C

**Answer option 1:** Abraham's hope helped him reason that Jehovah could resurrect Isaac if necessary. Hebrews 11:19 says he considered that God was able to raise him up even from the dead. That hope gave him courage to obey. In our case, focusing on the resurrection and the new world can help us face loss, fear, and difficult assignments with confidence.

**Answer option 2:** Hope gives courage because it helps us see beyond the immediate pain. Abraham was not focused only on the mountain, the knife, or the sacrifice. He was focused on Jehovah's promise. When we keep our hope clear, present trials do not disappear, but they become smaller compared with what Jehovah has promised.

---

### 7a. How might we imitate Abraham's courage and offer a sacrifice when we see an opportunity to give a witness? (Heb. 13:15)

**Answer option 1:** Hebrews 13:15 calls our words of praise a sacrifice. So when I see an opportunity to give a witness, I can imitate Abraham by offering Jehovah something even if it costs me comfort. It may cost courage, convenience, or the fear of what someone will think, but Jehovah values that sacrifice of praise.

**Answer option 2:** I can imitate Abraham by not waiting until witnessing feels easy. Abraham obeyed while the assignment felt difficult. In the same way, if an opportunity opens at work, school, or with family, I can say something simple about Jehovah. Even a brief witness can be a sacrifice that shows faith.

---

### 7b. How might we imitate Abraham's courage and offer a sacrifice when we decide how to use our material things? (Prov. 3:9)

**Answer option 1:** Proverbs 3:9 encourages us to honor Jehovah with our valuable things. That means I can imitate Abraham by putting Jehovah first when deciding how to use money, time, possessions, or energy. Instead of giving Jehovah what is left over, I can plan to support worship, ministry, hospitality, and the needs of others.

**Answer option 2:** Abraham's sacrifice teaches me that love for Jehovah should touch what is precious to me. Material things are not wrong, but they should not become untouchable. If I can use something I have to honor Jehovah or help his people, doing so shows that my trust is in him, not in what I own.

---

### 7c. How might we imitate Abraham's courage and offer a sacrifice when we learn that fellow believers need our help? (Phil. 4:18)

**Answer option 1:** Philippians 4:18 shows that help given to fellow believers can be like an acceptable sacrifice to God. So when brothers or sisters need help, I can imitate Abraham by acting, not just feeling concern. That help may cost time, energy, or resources, but Jehovah views loving support as something pleasing.

**Answer option 2:** Abraham did not hold back when doing what Jehovah asked. In a smaller way, I can refuse to hold back when I know someone needs encouragement, a meal, transport, or practical help. It takes courage to inconvenience myself, but love makes the sacrifice worthwhile, and Jehovah notices it.

---

### 8. In what other ways might you imitate the courage shown by Abraham in this account?

**Answer option 1:** I can imitate Abraham by obeying even when I do not understand every detail. Abraham knew Jehovah well enough to trust him through a very difficult command. When I receive Bible-based direction or counsel, I may not see the whole outcome right away, but I can still trust Jehovah's wisdom and obey.

**Answer option 2:** I can also imitate Abraham by building faith before a test comes. Abraham's courage did not appear suddenly on Mount Moriah. It grew from years of seeing Jehovah's justice, mercy, and power. So I want to meditate now on Jehovah's past actions and promises, because that faith will support me when a hard test comes.

---

## Meditate on the Bigger Picture

### 9. What does this account teach me about Jehovah?

**Answer option 1:** This account teaches me that Jehovah understands sacrificial love. He knew exactly how dear Isaac was to Abraham, and later Jehovah himself gave his only-begotten Son for us. So Jehovah is not distant from pain or sacrifice. He knows what love costs, and that makes me want to draw closer to him.

**Answer option 2:** It also teaches me that Jehovah never asks for obedience without purpose. Abraham's test strengthened his faith and gave us a powerful picture of Jehovah's own love. Jehovah can turn even a painful test into something that reveals his qualities and strengthens millions of people later.

---

### 10. How does this account tie in with Jehovah's purposes?

**Answer option 1:** This account ties in with Jehovah's purpose because Isaac was part of the line leading to the promised offspring. Jehovah confirmed that through Abraham's offspring all nations would be blessed. That promise ultimately points to Jesus and the blessings that will come through God's Kingdom.

**Answer option 2:** The account also helps us understand Jehovah's greatest act of love. Abraham was willing to offer his beloved son, and Jehovah later actually offered his Son, Jesus. So this event is not just about Abraham's courage. It points forward to the ransom and to Jehovah's purpose to bless obedient mankind forever.

---

### 11. What would I like to ask Abraham or Isaac in the resurrection?

**Answer option 1:** I would like to ask Abraham what he thought about during the walk to Moriah. Did he keep repeating Jehovah's promises in his mind? I would like to hear how hope helped him put one foot in front of the other when the assignment felt almost impossible.

**Answer option 2:** I would like to ask Isaac what he remembered about his father's faith that day. He was old enough to understand much of what was happening, yet he submitted. I would like to know how that experience affected his own trust in Jehovah and his respect for Abraham.

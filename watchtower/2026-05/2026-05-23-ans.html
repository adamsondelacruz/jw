How Will You Fight Your Distractions?

The Watchtower — March 2026  |  May 18–24, 2026
Song 35 & 129  |  "Keep perceiving what the will of Jehovah is." — Ephesians 5:17

1-2.How could something important become a distraction?

ANS1:Even good and necessary things — family, work, education, health — can become distractions when they take the priority spot reserved for Jehovah's Kingdom. Matthew 6:33 doesn't say "seek ONLY the Kingdom" — it says "seek FIRST." The danger isn't usually sin; it's misplaced order. A candle isn't bad, but placed in front of the sun, it blocks the greater light.

ANS2:This teaches us something tender about Jehovah: he doesn't ask us to abandon our responsibilities — he asks us to put HIM first within them. (Matthew 6:33) When Jehovah holds first place, every other "important thing" finds its proper place. In ministry, this answers the common objection "I'm too busy" — we don't need MORE time, we need ordered time.

3.Why will considering how to fight distractions help us?

ANS1:Because distractions rarely announce themselves — they arrive disguised as priorities. Anticipating them in advance is like a spiritual immune system. When we have already identified the threats, we respond by training rather than react in panic. Jesus himself prepared his disciples in advance for tests so they wouldn't be caught off guard.

ANS2:It also strengthens our ministry. A focused, joyful publisher is the strongest argument that serving Jehovah brings clarity in a noisy world. Householders may not remember our explanations, but they remember our peace. Considering distractions in advance protects the witness we give simply by how we live.

What Distractions Do We Face?

4-6.What kind of things could easily distract us?

ANS1:Luke 21:34-36 names the main culprits: overeating, heavy drinking, and "anxieties of life." Notice the balance — two are excesses of pleasure, one is the weight of worry. Satan attacks from both sides. Jesus warns that these can weigh down our hearts so the day of Jehovah overtakes us "suddenly as a snare."

ANS2:This teaches that Jehovah warns us SPECIFICALLY. (Luke 21:34-36) A loving Father doesn't just say "be careful" — he names the dangers. In ministry, we can share this insight: God doesn't leave his servants guessing about what to watch out for. The specifics of his warnings prove how much he wants us to make it through.

How Did Jesus Fight Potential Distractions?

7.What could have distracted Jesus?

ANS1:Jesus faced relentless distraction — Satan's direct temptations (Matthew 4:10), the constant demands of busy ministry (Matthew 9:35), and even Peter's well-meaning rebuke that contradicted God's will (Matthew 16:23). Notice: distractions can come from enemies AND from people who love us. Even Peter's affection nearly pulled Jesus off course.

ANS2:This teaches us that faithfulness doesn't mean freedom from distraction — even God's own Son faced it. (Matthew 4:10; 16:23) If Jesus actively had to fight distractions, we shouldn't be surprised when we face the same battle. The struggle itself isn't failure; only surrender is. That's encouraging news for any brother or sister fighting to stay focused.

8.How did Jesus fight potential distractions?

ANS1:He anchored himself in his Father's identity and will. John 8:28 — "I do nothing of my own initiative." John 14:9 — "He that has seen me has seen the Father." John 4:34 — "My food is to do the will of him who sent me." Jesus didn't merely tolerate God's will; he was nourished by it.

ANS2:This teaches the secret to fighting distractions: don't view God's will as a burden but as FOOD. (John 4:34) When doing Jehovah's will is what nourishes you, distractions don't feel like missed opportunities — they feel like empty calories. This is what we want our students to taste: that obedience isn't sacrifice, it's satisfaction.

How Can We Fight Our Distractions?

9.What does it mean to "keep perceiving what the will of Jehovah is"? (Ephesians 5:17)

ANS1:The word "keep" is the key — it's continuous, not one-time. (Ephesians 5:17) It's not enough to MEMORIZE Jehovah's will once; we must actively PERCEIVE it in every new situation. Like checking a compass on a long journey, we keep aligning our decisions, plans, and reactions with what we know pleases him.

ANS2:This teaches that Jehovah wants thinking servants, not robots. (Ephesians 5:17) He didn't write rules for every possible situation — instead, he gave principles and trained our discernment. This is liberating in our ministry: Jehovah doesn't demand blind obedience; he invites us to LEARN to think like him.

10.How can we learn about Jehovah's way of thinking?

ANS1:Through Scripture and prayer. Isaiah 55:9 reminds us his thoughts are higher than ours, but Psalm 143:10 — "teach me to do your will" — shows he WANTS to share his thinking with us. Daily reading, meditation, and prayer gradually transform our pattern of thinking until we instinctively respond as he would.

ANS2:This teaches that closeness to Jehovah comes through learning, not osmosis. (Psalm 143:10) He doesn't dump knowledge into our heads — he TEACHES us. The word "teach" implies a student-learner relationship: humble, regular, willing. In ministry, this is what we want to convey: Jehovah is the Master Teacher who patiently shapes minds.

11.What does Jehovah want for us?

ANS1:Three beautiful texts give the answer. 1 John 5:14 — he hears us when we ask according to his will. 1 Timothy 2:4 — he wants ALL kinds of people to be saved. Isaiah 48:17 — he teaches us "to benefit yourself." Every desire of Jehovah's heart for us is for our welfare — never against it.

ANS2:This is one of the most freeing truths in Scripture: Jehovah's commands aren't restrictions on our joy — they ARE our joy. (Isaiah 48:17) When a householder asks "Why does God forbid certain things?" the answer is simple: because he loves us. A Father who wants what's best sometimes says "no" — but always with a deeper "yes" behind it.

12.What is one way we can cope with anxiety about world events? (Matthew 5:3)

ANS1:By being "conscious of our spiritual need." (Matthew 5:3) When we feed the spiritual person, the worried mind quiets. Jesus added: don't be anxious about basic needs — your Father knows. (Matthew 6:31-32) Throwing our burden on Jehovah (Psalm 55:22) and trusting him completely (Proverbs 3:5-7) anchors us when the world rocks.

ANS2:This teaches that anxiety isn't conquered by denial but by REDIRECTION. (Matthew 5:3) Jesus didn't say "ignore your problems" — he said be conscious of a deeper need. When spiritual hunger is fed, fear loses its grip. In ministry, this is gold: people today don't need more news, they need more Jehovah.

13.How can we make "the best use of [our] time"?

ANS1:Ephesians 5:15-16 — "Walk carefully... making the best use of your time, because the days are wicked." "Best use" means choosing the BEST option, not just a good one. Time is our most non-renewable resource — every hour spent is gone forever. So we ask: of all I COULD do right now, what would Jehovah value most?

ANS2:This teaches that Jehovah values our time so much that he commands us to guard it. (Ephesians 5:15-16) He won't spend it for us — that's our stewardship. In a world designed to fragment our attention into a thousand pieces, the simple willingness to focus on Kingdom interests becomes an act of worship.

14.In what way does staying busy in spiritual activities help us?

ANS1:Psalm 16:8 — "I have placed Jehovah in front of me constantly... I will never be shaken." Psalm 112:1, 6-8 — the righteous one is "not afraid of bad news; his heart is firm." Spiritual activity isn't a distraction FROM life — it is the anchor IN life. The busier we are with Jehovah's work, the steadier we stand.

ANS2:This teaches that the human mind is always engaged with SOMETHING — the question is what. (Psalm 16:8) If we fill our minds with spiritual matters, there's no vacuum for fear or temptation to fill. This is why ministry is therapeutic — every door we knock on pulls us into Jehovah's purpose and out of our own anxieties.

15.What will being "sound in mind" enable us to do? (1 Peter 4:7)

ANS1:1 Peter 4:7 — "Be sound in mind, and be vigilant with a view to prayers." It enables us to think clearly under pressure and pray with focus. 2 Timothy 1:7 reminds us God gave us "a spirit of power, of love, and of soundness of mind." Soundness aligns our thinking with reality and with Jehovah's standards, even when emotions try to override.

ANS2:This teaches that fear is not from Jehovah. (2 Timothy 1:7) In our ministry, a sound mind shows. People can tell when we speak from peace versus panic. Our composure under uncertainty becomes our most persuasive witness — because in a world losing its mind, the brother or sister who is calm is the one strangers want to listen to.

16.What did Jesus focus on as his death approached?

ANS1:He focused on his Father and his assignment. In Gethsemane, his first response to overwhelming sorrow was PRAYER. (Luke 22:39-46) "Not my will, but yours take place." Then on the stake, his final words: "It has been accomplished!" (John 19:30) Jesus' last focus was completing what Jehovah had given him to do.

ANS2:This teaches us the most powerful prayer principle, modeled by Jesus himself: when overwhelmed, pray HARDER. (Luke 22:39-46) Jesus didn't avoid pain by escape — he metabolized it through prayer. And then he finished his work. (John 19:30) For us, the model is clear: don't quit when distracted by suffering — bring it to Jehovah, and finish.

17.Why do many use social media, but how could it be a distraction?

ANS1:People use it to connect, learn, or pass time. But Proverbs 4:25 warns: "Let your eyes look straight ahead" — social media tempts us to look EVERYWHERE except forward. And Matthew 6:22 — the eye is "the lamp of the body" — what we constantly view shapes what we become. Hours of scrolling are hours not invested in eternity.

ANS2:This teaches that Jehovah cares about our attention, not just our actions. (Matthew 6:22) The simple, focused eye lights the whole body; the divided eye gathers darkness. Our phones aren't evil — but if they steal the focus that should be Jehovah's, they become quiet idols that demand worship one glance at a time.

18.Why do we need good judgment when selecting entertainment?

ANS1:Philippians 1:10 — "make sure of the more important things... and not be stumbling others." Entertainment isn't neutral — it shapes our heart, our conscience, and our example. Good judgment asks: does this help me discern the important, or does it dull my spiritual senses? What I watch when no one is looking is forming the person Jehovah sees.

ANS2:This teaches us that Jehovah trusts us to think. (Philippians 1:10) He gives few specific rules about entertainment because he wants us to develop discernment — a heart that instinctively chooses what is right. The more we exercise this judgment, the more Jehovah-like our taste becomes. And our example protects others' consciences too.

19.What can happen if we center our life on recreation?

ANS1:1 Timothy 6:19 urges us to "store up safely... a fine foundation for the future, so that they may get a firm hold on the real life." Recreation is fine, but when it becomes the CENTER, we trade the "real life" for a counterfeit. We end up with an entertaining life but no foundation — full of moments, empty of meaning.

ANS2:This teaches that Jehovah distinguishes between the "real life" and a counterfeit. (1 Timothy 6:19) Recreation gives momentary joy but cannot give meaning. A life centered on fun LOOKS alive but is spiritually shallow. Jehovah invites us to something deeper — a firm hold on the life that truly is life.

20.How do we benefit by fighting distractions?

ANS1:Matthew 24:44 — "Keep ready, because at an hour that you do not think likely, the Son of man is coming." Fighting distractions keeps us ready. The benefit isn't just spiritual survival — it's the joy of arriving at Jehovah's day with hands full of work done, not full of regret over what we wasted.

ANS2:This teaches that Jehovah rewards focused servants. (Matthew 24:44) Every distraction defeated is a step closer to him and a stronger spiritual core. In a fragmented world, our undistracted faith stands out — and often becomes the very thing that makes a sincere seeker ask, "What is it that you have that I don't?"

How Can You Fight Distractions By...

...Learning to think like Jehovah?

ANS1:By regular Bible reading and meditation that gradually train our minds to react the way he would. (Isaiah 55:9; Psalm 143:10) When his thoughts become our defaults, distractions lose their pull because our minds are already preoccupied with what matters most.

ANS2:It's not about gaining more knowledge — it's about gaining HIS perspective. (1 John 5:14) The more we see life through Jehovah's eyes, the smaller distractions appear in comparison to the privilege of doing his will. We stop measuring time in hours and start measuring it in eternity.

...Staying busy in spiritual activities?

ANS1:Psalm 16:8 — keep Jehovah "in front of you constantly." A busy spiritual life leaves no vacuum for distraction to fill. Ministry, meetings, personal study, and prayer create a rhythm that protects the heart from the world's pull and keeps us close to our Father.

ANS2:Spiritual activity isn't merely an obligation — it's a SHIELD. (Psalm 112:1, 6-8) The brother or sister immersed in Kingdom work is the one least shaken by bad news. Their focus has become their fortress. The world's storms still come, but they don't reach the inner room where Jehovah dwells.

...Keeping a clear view of what is truly important?

ANS1:By regularly asking, "What would Jehovah value most right now?" (Philippians 1:10) Time spent on the "more important things" is time invested in eternity. Distractions lose their appeal when we keep the prize in clear sight — and when our heart is fixed on what matters to him.

ANS2:This teaches us that clarity is a DAILY choice, not a one-time decision. (Matthew 24:44) Every day we either grow clearer or more cluttered. Choosing to keep ready is choosing to be the kind of servant Jehovah can use in any moment — and that readiness itself becomes its own reward.

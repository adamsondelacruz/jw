Microphones

7. Polar Patterns. A polar pattern is the sensitivity the microphone has to sound coming
from each direction. The microphones provided use the Super Cardioid polar pattern, the
Hyper Cardioid polar pattern or the Cardioid polar pattern. In all of these cases, the top of
the microphone should be directed towards the person speaking, and at a distance of 10 to
15 cm for the best audio quality.
  (1) Super Cardioid Polar Pattern. This style of microphone is very good at rejecting
      noise from the side of the microphone.




  (2) Hyper Cardioid Polar Pattern. This is essentially the same as a super cardioid polar
      pattern, but with a narrower pickup range.
  (3) Cardioid Polar Pattern. This style of microphone is very good at rejecting sound
      from directly behind the microphone.

Stage Boxes

11. A stage box is a remote interface device used to connect equipment to a mixing console.
It provides an on-stage central hub for connecting microphones, instruments, and powered
speakers or amplifiers to a multicore cable snake or a digital cable. This allows the sound
desk to be further from the stage. Instead of having to route several cables across a long
distance, only one cable needs to be routed. Analogue stage boxes don’t convert the signal,
just transport it through a multi-core cable. Digital stage boxes convert the signal from ana-
logue to digital, and then transport the digital signal, usually via ethernet or fibre optic cables.

Audio Mixers

24. Adjusting gain using solo and the LED meter. When setting up a mixer, it is best to
adjust the gain for each input to ensure a good signal level with the channel fader at unity.
The audio level is displayed on the mixer’s LED meter, which differs for digital and analogue
mixers. The goal when adjusting the input gain is to have the audio level averaging around
-20 for a digital mixer and around +4 for an analogue mixer. To perform this adjustment:
  (1) Have the output faders lowered.
  (2) Press SOLO on the channel being adjusted.
  (3) Start with the microphone inputs. Have someone with a loud voice read a passage to
      generate audio into the microphone for that channel.
      • If the mixer has a pre-fader level (PFL) solo mode, the fader can be left down. If
        PFL can’t be enabled on a given mixer, raise the channel’s fader to unity (0).
  (4) Adjust the input gain knob clockwise until the meter reaches around the target level.
      • Avoid turning the input to gain to maximum. This can cause noise or hiss.
      • Even with the gain almost maxed, the audio level might still not be at the desired
        target level. If this happens, take note of the level the microphone can reasonably
        achieve, and use this level as the basis for adjusting the other gain knobs.

  (5) Adjust the input gain for the media inputs. With pink noise or prelude music playing,
      and the computer volume set at 80%, adjust the input gain knob clockwise until the
      meter reaches around the same target level as the microphones.
  (6) The topmost light in the LED meter indicates channel clipping (it might be labelled
      ‘clip’ or display a number to indicate the clipping level). If this LED ever illuminates
      during testing, reduce the input gain for the active channel.

25. Adjusting Mixer Output Levels. Different pieces of audio equipment have different
input and output level requirements. Depending on what equipment is next in the audio
chain, the input or output levels might need adjustment. If the next device expects a signal
level significantly lower than the mixer is outputting, it might clip or distort the sound. If the
next device is expecting a signal level significantly higher, to compensate, it may need to
add gain and perhaps introduce hiss or noise into the system. Work through each device in
the signal chain to ensure the signal is neither too high nor too low for the next device. One
way to achieve this is:
  (1) Connect each audio device in the chain.
       • Mixer to Processors, Processors to Amplifiers, Amplifiers to speakers.
       • Some of these devices may be integrated or pre-managed in a headend or rack,
         not needing to be connected during setup.
  (2) Make sure all amplifiers are turned down.
  (3) Bypass all processing (Graphic EQ, DSP). Note: Once these are engaged, gain may
      need to be raised slightly to compensate, if possible.
  (4) Have someone read a passage in a loud voice, or play prelude music.
  (5) Raise the fader for that input channel to Unity (0).
  (6) Slowly raise the master fader to Unity (0).
       • At some venues inhouse amplification is used, and some of these have the ampli-
         fication set to a predefined level they don’t want adjusted. In these cases, the out-
         put faders will need to be adjusted to whatever level suits the inhouse system.
  (7) Check that the signal lights on the equipment in line after the mixer are also showing
      good levels.
       • These should match the levels shown on the mixer meter, and not be clipping.
       • The fader might need adjustment at this point to ensure the signal going out is at
         the level needed by the next device.
  (8) Slowly raise the amplifier or speaker level.
  (9) Listen for excessive noise, hiss, or any distortion.
  (10) If there are any issues:
       • Check each device again to see if any are clipping, or getting too low a signal.
       • Check if any of the gain knobs are maxed out or close to being maxed out. (this
         can cause issues, just dial it back slightly).

28. Input Section – Fader Area.
  (1) The Fader. This controls the volume of the input.
  (2) The Mas, 1-2, 3-4 mono and stereo buttons. These buttons can be used
      to make an input be sent to only a subset of the output channels. For exam-
      ple, some inputs may not need to be sent back to the stage monitors. In
      normal operation these three buttons will all be pressed in.
  (3) The Solo button. This makes the LED meter on the top right display the
      levels for this input only. It can be useful when adjusting the volume for an
      input.
  (4) The Mute button. This mutes the input.
  (5) The pan control. This controls the degree to which the input feeds the left
      channel, the right channel, or both. For inputs, leave this at 0 (feeding both
      left and right channels).

29. Auxiliary Send Controls. There are six auxiliary send outputs. There are mon
1 and 2 (1), aux 1 and 2 (2), and aux 3 and 4 (3). On each input there are knobs that
control if the input will be sent to the given auxiliary channel.
  (1) Mon channels. These can be configured for pre or post fader operation per
      input. Press the button in to enable this behaviour. These output to the mon-
      itor 1 and monitor 2 outputs on the rear of the audio mixer.
  (2) Aux channels. These can be configured for pre or post fader operation (see
      the output section for more information).

30. Audio Equalising Controls. These are useful for creating natural sounding
voices, ensuring high quality audio from media, and reducing problems with feed-
back and pops or sibilance.
  (1) EQ on. The equalisation settings will only activate if the red eq button is
      pressed in.
  (2) EQ knobs. Each ring has two knobs. The outer knob selects a frequency,
      and the inner adjusts the audio/decibel level around the selected frequency.
      With each knob pointed up at 12 o’clock, there will be no effect on the sound.
      There are four targetable frequencies per input on the mixer. Avoid being
      overly aggressive with the equalisation settings, as this can result in the au-
      dio sounding unnatural.
  (3) Mic phase switch. This causes a 180-degree phase change in the input
      signal, reversing the polarity. This is commonly needed where two micro-
      phones are used facing each other. Ordinarily the two microphones would
      be out of phase causing cancellation when the console sums the two signals
      into the output. Reversing the phase of one signal causes the microphones
      to be out of phase with each other and no have no cancellation.
  (4) 80Hz. Enabling this attenuates any audio signal below the 80Hz frequency.
  (5) Gain. This adjusts the input amplifier gain before the signal is adjusted by the fader.
  (6) +48v / phantom power button. Never press this button when the fader is up.
      This button activates phantom power to the XLR connector. See the microphones
      section for information about which microphones need phantom power.

Power Boards and Cables

7. Power Boards. The Kit contains several different power boards for use. Avoid chaining
power boards together as that risks overloading the boards and creates a fire risk. Avoid
placing power boards in high traffic zones as they can be a trip hazard. Each power board
in the kit can safely handle up to 10A 2400W. If the power board suddenly stops working, it
could be that it was overloaded and the board’s circuit breaker tripped. If this happens look
for the black button at the base of the board, and try pressing it in to reset the breaker.




8. Power Extension Cables: The kit comes with extension cables of different lengths. To
avoid safety risks, do not connect multiple cables to extend their length. Instead, opt for the
longer cables provided, which are designed for safe current transmission over extended
distances. If extension cables must be run across areas with foot traffic, use the included
cable protectors to prevent trip hazards.

down one channel at a time, and allowing the output to be read.
   (1) To use, connect the cable to be tested to both appropriate connections on the tester.
   (2) Cycle the knob to test each conductor (1-3 for a three pin cable, 1-5 for a five pin,
       etc.).
   (3) If any conductor does not activate the orange light, it means there is a broken con-
       ductor or connector. If any conductor activates two orange lights, one conductor is
       shorting into another. In either case, mark the cable as defective and do not use it.

INTERPRETATION OPERATION

11. The interpreters and mixer operator will sit at a table. Each interpreter will be provided
with a broadcast headset. These headsets will be connected to a headphone amplifier that
allows each interpreter to individually adjust the volume at which he or she hears the pro-
gram. Each headset also has an attached microphone. Place a video monitor on the table
so that both the interpreters and the mixer operator can see the convention program as it is
displayed to the audience.

12. This system supports three simultaneous interpreters:
   (2) The primary interpreter translates for the chairman and for those who give talks.
   (3) The male interpreter translates for other male program participants, such as those
       who deliver interviews, soliloquies, or demonstrations.
   (4) The female interpreter translates for all female program participants.

13. The mixer operator will not hear the primary convention language. His headphones will
be connected only to the mixer. This will allow him to carefully monitor and adjust the level
and equalization of the interpreters. When music is played, he will raise the mixer input that
receives audio from the primary convention sound system. When videos are played, he will
raise the mixer input that receives the alternate language feed from the primary media player.

14. The mixer operator should keep levels balanced throughout the program. Interpreter
microphones should be turned off when the interpreter is not speaking.

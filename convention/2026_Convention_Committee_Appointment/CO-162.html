CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION


1. Convention Committees can use JW Stream to provide the program to publishers unable
to attend in person and to departments within the venue when approved by the branch office.

2. When providing the program to departments within the venue, a direct cable connec-
tion from the video desk remains the preferred option. If it is feasible to run a cable, this
should be done to ensure a stable and high-quality feed. Streaming should be used for de-
partments when a direct connection is not practical.

3. This document outlines the policy and procedural responsibilities as well as technical in-
formation for Convention Committees, audio/video overseers, and information technology
overseers.

STREAMING CONFIGURATIONS

1. Streaming can be configured in two ways:
   (1) Onsite Streaming: Used for internal needs within the venue, such as isolated depart-
       ments requiring access to the program.
   (2) Offsite Streaming: Provides access for publishers unable to attend in person. Elders
       will have the ability to share a link with such persons.

2. The Service Department determines whether a convention program will be streamed and
which configuration is permitted. (Depending on the situation, both configurations may be
permitted.) Convention Committees should wait for confirmation of this direction before
making any arrangements.

3. The Local Broadcasting Department (LBD) manages streaming configurations. Direct any
technical questions to the LBD.

STREAMING METHODS

4. There are two video streaming methods available for livestreams of local conventions:
broadcast stream and real-time stream. Broadcast streams are slightly delayed and incur a
low data cost for the organization. Links generated by elders to send to publishers are used
to view broadcast streams. Real-time streams have no discernible delay. They incur a higher
data cost for the organization than broadcast streams.

5. In order to save dedicated funds, use broadcast streams whenever possible. Only 25 real-
time connections are available for each convention. The following examples are acceptable
uses for real-time streams.—See 3:4.
   (1) To provide a live program feed to the chairman’s office or the Convention Committee
       office, if a wired feed is not practical. The brothers working in these offices must be
       able to monitor the program in real time.
   (2) To provide a live program feed to a department located in an area where the loud-
       speakers from the main auditorium can be heard.

CONVENTION COMMITTEE

6. The Convention Committee makes sure that the streaming arrangements are in harmony
with direction from the branch office and that all responsible brothers understand how the ar-
rangements should be handled.

7. The Convention Committee should work with department overseers to ensure that volun-
teers who view broadcast streams enter “0” in the attendance field when they connect on

AUDIO/VIDEO OVERSEER

8. The audio/video overseer works closely with the information technology overseer to en-
sure that they have everything needed to set up and manage the stream effectively.

CONGREGATION ELDERS

9. Elders ensure that the stream link is provided to those in their congregation in harmony
with the latest direction from the organization. They arrange to provide technical assistance
as needed.

STREAMING PREPARATION TIMELINE

10. After the Service Department has sent the approved streaming configurations to the
Convention Committee
   (1) The audio/video (AV) overseer ensures that the AV equipment needed to send a
       stream will be in place.
   (2) The AV overseer assigns the information technology (IT) overseer the “Manager” role
       on JW Stream.—See 3:3.
   (3) The IT overseer assigns the “Streamer” role to brothers who are responsible to mon-
       itor the stream.—See 3:3.
   (4) The IT overseer creates a schedule of brothers who will be assigned to monitor the
       streaming computer during the program. Because of the way that JW Stream is ac-
       cessed, one brother should monitor the stream for the full day so that there is no need
       to switch logins between brothers.

11. No more than 30 days before the start of the convention
   (1) The AV overseer confirms that the visibility setting for the event in JW Stream is set to
       “Visible” for each event and language that is approved for streaming.—See 3:2.

12. One week before the start of the convention
   (1) The IT overseer confirms that all key personnel are assigned the correct roles on
       JW Stream and verifies that all brothers responsible to monitor the stream can log in
       and view the stream meeting interface.—See 3:3.
   (2) The IT overseer creates and distributes a real-time link for each department approved
       by the Convention Committee.

13. During setup day
   (1) A member of the AV Department sets up the stream computer. It should be located
       close to the video desk when possible. If the Internet connection is located a long dis-
       tance from the video desk, an effort should be made to provide a long network cable
       (as opposed to a long SDI cable) so the IT crew can be located close to the rest of the
       brothers in the AV Department. A wired Internet connection is preferred for the

HANDLING TECHNICAL ISSUES

13. Direct technical questions to the Local Broadcasting Department, which manages
streaming configurations.

14. JW Stream allows a connection to both a primary and backup computer. This feature al-
lows the backup feed to immediately display to the audience in case of failure. When the
broadcast stream goes live, the primary and backup roles cannot be changed on the
JW Stream interface unless the broadcast stream is stopped.

15. If both the primary and backup computers are disconnected from the meeting, remote
viewers will see a black screen. If the primary or backup computer is reconnected without
stopping the stream, viewers will see the stream again. If the broadcast stream is stopped,
they will see a screen that informs them that they are connected and that the stream has not
started. When the broadcast stream is restarted, a “Play Now” button will appear. Viewers
can select “Play Now” to resume watching the stream.

16. When stream issues occur, identify whether hardware or network problems caused the
disruption. Minor problems caused by intermittent network issues, such as the stream stut-
tering every so often, may be tolerable. Hardware issues, such as computer or SDI ingest
device failure, may require the following troubleshooting steps:
   (1) Both primary and backup streaming computers equipped with SDI ingest devices:
         When the primary computer fails and the backup computer continues to function,
          select the “Send to stage” icon on the backup computer to make it the active
          source.
         If both computers fail or become disconnected, reboot both computers. Log in to
          the meeting again on both computers. Stop the broadcast stream. Restart the
          stream.
   (2) Both the primary and backup streaming computer connected to the meeting but only
       the primary has an SDI ingest device:
         When the primary computer fails and the backup computer continues to be con-
          nected to the meeting, disconnect the SDI ingest device from the primary computer
          and connect it to the backup computer.
         On the backup computer, select the gear icon and select the SDI ingest device as
          the camera and microphone.
         Select the “Send to stage” icon on the backup computer to make it the active
          source.
   (3) If you have only one streaming computer:
         When the streaming computer fails and the network continues to function, restart
          the computer if possible. If necessary, locate a backup computer.

17. Log in to JW Stream and navigate to the meeting. Stop the broadcast stream. Restart
the stream. Sometimes, in rare cases, the venue’s Internet connection gives the Windows
computer direct access to the public Internet. For security reasons, this is not ideal. An easy
way to reduce risk is to connect the computer through a simple, locally purchased home
router instead of connecting it directly. You can determine if the Windows computer is con-
nected directly to the public Internet by checking to see if its assigned IP address is on a
public or private subnet.

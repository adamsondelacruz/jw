Departments under the rooming overseer’s oversight

CO-1’s standard organisation chart assigns six functions to the rooming overseer. The local chart presently shows three department groups. Installation and Trucking and Equipment are likely unnecessary at an Assembly Hall unless local needs require them. (CO-1 1:10; 2:75; 3:55, 80.)

Cleaning

Responsible brothers: Ramon Ruiz (overseer); Charles Garcia and Ivander Montemayor (assistants).

In plain language, Cleaning keeps the venue ready for worship before, throughout, and after the program.

Main responsibilities from CO-1 3:33-41:

  • Write congregations in advance with any assigned auditorium seating-section cleaning and ask each body of elders to choose a capable exemplary coordinator.
  • Recruit department volunteers for corridors, windows, offices, restrooms, exterior grounds, and other common areas.
  • Work with the rooming overseer on any preconvention cleaning; invite congregations to support it when required.
  • Clean considerately after sessions, allowing attendees reasonable opportunity for fellowship and handling requests to move in a loving way.
  • Protect people from slips or similar hazards when cleaning spills or floors.
  • Use contract-supplied cleaning/restroom supplies where applicable; secure them and inventory them at the beginning and end of each day.
  • Train restroom cleaners in chemical use and provide protective gloves.
  • Assign restroom monitors to restock supplies, detect spills/plumbing failures, and prevent children from playing there.
  • Manage rubbish safely and recycle where required by law.
  • At the convention’s end, report total supplies used to the contract representative.

Rooming overseer handoff: Agree on preconvention cleaning, verify staffing and safety readiness, and visit the department daily with Daveson Asuncion. The Convention Committee coordinator, not hotel/facility staff approached directly, handles contract-supplied cleaning shortages through the correct route.

Information and Volunteer Service / Lost & Found

Responsible brothers: Jeremeias Meru (overseer); Luis Limaco (assistant).

This combined local department is the public help point for directions and approved volunteer placement, and the secure point for lost property, lost persons, and any checkroom function used locally.

Information responsibilities — CO-1 3:51

  • Recruit exemplary brothers and sisters who can explain department locations and clearly direct people to hotels, shops, restaurants, pharmacies, and other useful locations.
  • Give every department volunteer a copy of the simple convention department directory supplied by the rooming overseer.
  • Do not give the internal directories to attendees generally.
  • Do not set up convention information boards.

Volunteer Service responsibilities — CO-1 3:52-54

  • When a department remains understaffed, help prepare one consolidated request to the bodies of elders rather than having several departments send separate letters.
  • Clearly state which departments need help and the qualifications required.
  • Ask the congregation secretary for approved volunteers’ names, contact information, and department choices.
  • Forward those details to the relevant department overseer for follow-up.
  • During the convention, verify a person’s approval by his or her congregation elders before directing the person to a department needing help.
  • Never use this process to request volunteers for Accounts.

Lost & Found / Checkroom responsibilities — CO-1 3:63-67

  • Staff the function whenever the facility is open.
  • Display inexpensive items, but securely hold wallets, purses, badge cards, and expensive items; claimants must identify them.
  • Bring separated individuals—especially children—to this department, care for them appropriately, and notify Attendant and the Convention Committee. Do not make a lost-person announcement.
  • After the convention, hold remaining items under the department overseer. Return accurately identified items at the claimant’s expense.
  • Give unclaimed cash to Accounts at the end of the convention.
  • After two months, send still-unclaimed significant-value items to the Convention and Assembly Desk; do not send songbooks, Bibles, or low-value items.
  • If a checkroom is used locally, label checked articles and allow extra space on the final day.

Rooming overseer handoff: Supply the department directories. If volunteer gaps remain, ask Jeremeias Meru to draft the consolidated letter with input from the affected departments, then obtain the required approval before sending it.

Rooming Department

Responsible brothers: Meliton Soriano (overseer); Romel Lumiguid (assistant).

In plain language, this team operates the on-site accommodation help desk. It helps attendees with rooming problems or last-minute needs and sends matters outside routine authority to Adamson Dela Cruz.

Main responsibilities from CO-1 1:23-25; 2:58, 71; 3:78-79:

  • Set up a conveniently located Rooming desk; consider a second desk only if convention size warrants it.
  • Use the assistant overseer(s) and keymen to handle routine desk matters.
  • Escalate unresolved or exceptional matters to the rooming overseer.
  • Keep current approved accommodation and contact information available without exposing confidential data.
  • Support approved Special Rooming Needs Requests (CO-5a), including circuit overseers and others in special full-time service.
  • Maintain readiness for private-home accommodation when complimentary hotel rooms are insufficient, following CO-80.
  • Remember that the full Convention Committee makes complimentary-accommodation assignments.
  • For unusual rooming situations, work through the rooming overseer to the rooming coordinator.

Required next source: CO-80 contains the detailed operating procedure and must be obtained and studied before these arrangements are finalised.

Installation (if needed — not shown on the local chart)

CO-1 says Installation will likely not be needed at an Assembly Hall. If formed, it handles convention setup, maintenance, repair, move-out, signs, and any permitted stage construction or decoration. Volunteers must meet facility-contract and legal requirements. Nonstandard signs require the rooming overseer’s approval. The Convention Committee coordinator submits any CO-100 sign request after consulting the installation overseer. (2:75, 77; 3:55-62.)

Local status: No overseer or assistant appears on the supplied chart. Confirm with the Assembly Hall overseer and Convention Committee that local needs are fully covered without a separate department.

Trucking and Equipment (if needed — not shown on the local chart)

CO-1 says this department will likely not be needed at an Assembly Hall. If formed, it locates and transports convention equipment; records borrowed equipment on CO-30; records pickup/return movements on CO-31; checks equipment before accepting it; confirms driver licences, experience, vehicle credentials, and insurance; and reports accidents immediately. Rental or purchase requests go to the rooming overseer, and the Convention Committee must obtain branch approval before proceeding. (2:25; 3:80-85; Appendix F.)

Local status: No overseer or assistant appears on the supplied chart. Confirm whether the Assembly Hall’s existing arrangements cover all equipment movement needs.

Oversight rhythm

  • Several months before: personnel should be selected early enough to recruit keymen/volunteers and organise fully. (1:33.)
  • Two to three months before: know the status of each department and correct staffing shortages. Participate in the committee’s preconvention meeting with department overseers and assistants. (1:11; 2:31.)
  • Convention week: ensure the rooming desk and department locations are ready; distribute directories. (1:25-26; Appendix E.)
  • Every convention day: Adamson Dela Cruz and Daveson Asuncion should visit each supervised department, commend good work, and assist with improvement. (1:12; Appendix E.)
  • After the convention: participate in the CO-53 personnel review, preserve useful training information for next year, and ensure each department completes its specific handoff or retention work. (1:40-41; 2:8-11; 3:66.)

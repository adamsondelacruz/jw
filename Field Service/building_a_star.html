Building a STAR

Preparing the Ground Before You Read | Field Service
Key scriptures: Colossians 4:6 · Jeremiah 10:23

S — Situation · T — Think · A — Answer · R — Read
The goal: prepare the person's mind before reading the scripture, so it lands on prepared ground.

Introduction

(engaging) Have you ever been in a conversation where you're excited to share a scripture, but before you know it, you've read the verse and the person is looking at you like you've just read the terms and conditions of a software agreement? (pause for smiles)

Sometimes we're so eager to get to the scripture that we skip the preparation.

There's a saying — a good meal smells good before it tastes good. (beat) In the same way, a scripture often has more impact when we prepare the person's mind before we read it.

(read) Notice what Paul said at Colossians 4:6:

"Let your words always be gracious, seasoned with salt, so that you will know how you should answer each person."

That verse reminds us that our ministry is not one-size-fits-all. Different people have different concerns, different backgrounds, and different ways of thinking. Paul says we should know how to answer each person.

The Method: Building a STAR

(enthusiastic) One simple way to do that is by building a STAR. Yes, you heard it right — building a STAR.

LetterStep

Let's see how that works with Jeremiah 10:23.

S — Situation

First we build an S — Situation.

You might say:

"Many people today are concerned about the direction the world is taking. We see rising costs, conflict, and uncertainty about the future."

You've connected with something that matters to them.

T — Think

Next, Think.

Ask a question that gets them thinking:

"Do you think humans are capable of successfully guiding their own direction?"

A — Answer

Now comes an important step. Answer.

Wait for the householder's answer.

(humor) In fact, that may be the hardest part of STAR! Sometimes we're so eager to share the scripture that we ask a question and then answer it ourselves before the householder has a chance.

But when we pause and listen, we learn what is on their mind.

R — Read

Then comes Read.

You might say:

"That's an interesting point. Notice what the Bible says at Jeremiah 10:23."

Then read the scripture:

"I well know, O Jehovah, that man's way does not belong to him. It does not belong to man who is walking even to direct his step."

Conclusion

Now, the person is already engaged. They've considered the question. They've expressed an opinion. They're waiting to see what the Bible says.

(slow, conclude) The scripture lands on prepared ground.
